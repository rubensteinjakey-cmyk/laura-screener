### How a company gets its score

1. The agent reads the company's **Business section (Item 1)**. Whether MD&A (Item 7) is added depends on the test run.
2. It scores each of the four themes from **0 to 5** using the ladders below.
3. The code turns the four theme scores into one **final score out of 100**:

| Theme | Weight |
|---|---|
| Creativity, art and storytelling | 35% |
| Community, education and collaboration | 30% |
| Cross-cultural connection and inclusion | 20% |
| Entrepreneurship (supporting founders and small businesses) | 15% |

> **Final score = (Creativity ÷ 5 × 35) + (Community ÷ 5 × 30) + (Cross-cultural ÷ 5 × 20) + (Entrepreneurship ÷ 5 × 15)**
>
> Example: Creativity 4, Community 3, Cross-cultural 2, Entrepreneurship 3 → 28 + 18 + 8 + 9 = **63 / 100**

4. Companies are ranked **within their sector**, so banks are compared with banks.

The screener is a values filter only. It helps choose among stocks that already fit the financial plan, and it never outweighs safely funding the $50,000 yearly payments for 2033–2042.

### Rules that apply to every theme

1. **Score actions, not claims.** Products, programs, partnerships, money spent and numbers reached count. Mission statements, value statements and "we are committed to…" language can never score above **1**.
2. **No quote, no points.** Any score of 1 or higher needs 1–3 quotes copied word for word from the filing (each under about 40 words).
3. **The code checks every quote.** If a quote doesn't appear in the filing word for word (ignoring extra spaces and line breaks), that theme's score becomes **0** and the company is flagged for a human to check.
4. **When torn between two levels, pick the lower one.**
5. **Skip hypothetical language.** "We may," "we plan to explore" and risk-style wording don't count. Only things the company says it is doing or has done count.
6. **Score each theme separately.** One program can count for two themes only if it really fits both (for example, an arts-education program fits Creativity and Community).
7. **Thin filing flag.** If the filing says too little to judge Community or Cross-cultural (for example, the human capital section is missing or under about 150 words), the agent sets `thin_filing = true`. The scores stay as they are for now. The company then gets an automated second look: the agent finds its latest sustainability report and rescores Community and Cross-cultural from it, where scores can only go up (see Step 9 in Section 5).
8. **Be politically neutral.** Two companies doing the same thing get the same score, whether or not they use labels like "DEI." A label with no actual practice behind it scores at most 1.

### Theme 1: Creativity, art and storytelling (35%)

**What it means:** the company creates, publishes or enables original creative work, or supports artists, writers, designers and storytellers as workers, customers or partners.

**What counts:** creative software and tools; publishing, film, music, games, animation, illustration; paying or licensing creators; marketplaces for creative work; arts sponsorships and arts-education programs.

| Score | What the 10-K shows | Illustration |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Nothing related to creative work | A pipeline operator |
| 1 | Only general language | "We foster a creative, innovative culture" |
| 2 | One specific creative-related practice, small or on the side | A bank that names one arts festival it sponsors |
| 3 | Several specific practices, or a named program with details | A named arts-education program with student numbers, or a described product line for designers that's a small part of the business |
| 4 | Creators or creative work are a major business segment or customer group, with measurable details | A tech company reporting how many creative professionals use its design products |
| 5 | Creating, publishing or enabling creative work is the core business | A book publisher, film studio or creative-software company |

**Traps (these don't count):** "state-of-the-art"; "design" meaning engineering or chip design; "licensing" of patents or drugs; "content" meaning recycled content or web content in general; "storytelling" as a marketing term for selling the brand; "creative" in ad-agency boilerplate with no creators involved.

### Theme 2: Community, education and collaboration (30%)

**What it means:** the company invests in teaching, mentoring and developing people, and partners with schools, libraries, nonprofits and local communities so people learn and create together.

**What counts:** education products and services; mentoring, apprenticeship and tuition programs with specifics; scholarships; school, library and nonprofit partnerships; community investment with dollar amounts; employee volunteering with numbers.

| Score | What the 10-K shows | Illustration |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Nothing related | — |
| 1 | Only general language or standard training | "We invest in our people and our communities"; "employees receive annual compliance training" |
| 2 | One specific practice | A tuition reimbursement program, or one named nonprofit partner |
| 3 | Several specific practices, or a named program with numbers | A mentorship program with participant counts plus a volunteering program with hours logged |
| 4 | A large, measurable commitment central to strategy, or education is a significant business line | Trains thousands of non-employees each year in skills programs |
| 5 | Education, learning or community-building is the core business | An education-technology or education-publishing company |

**Traps (these don't count):** "community bank"; "the investment / financial / medical / scientific community"; "collaboration agreement" or "partnership" meaning a biotech licensing deal or a legal partnership structure; "training" meaning AI model training or required compliance training; "educating customers" as marketing.

### Theme 3: Cross-cultural connection and inclusion (20%)

**What it means:** the company serves and includes people across cultures, languages and backgrounds, including immigrants, Asian American and LGBTQ+ communities, people with disabilities and other underrepresented groups. It's scored on **what the company does**, not what it calls it.

**What counts:** multilingual products and services; localization built for different cultures; products or services aimed at immigrant communities; accessibility features or products; employee resource groups; inclusive benefits; named programs for underrepresented groups; cultural exchange programs.

| Score | What the 10-K shows | Illustration |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Nothing related | — |
| 1 | General statements, or demographic statistics with no practice described | "We value a diverse workforce"; equal-opportunity boilerplate; workforce percentages only |
| 2 | One specific practice | Employee resource groups, or customer support in several languages |
| 3 | Several specific practices, or a named program with details | ERGs plus an accessibility program plus a named recruiting partnership |
| 4 | A meaningful part of the business, with measurable reach | Products available in many languages with stated user numbers, or accessibility as its own product line |
| 5 | Connecting people across cultures or serving immigrant or underserved communities is the core business | A language-learning or translation company, or a service built mainly for immigrant families |

**Traps (these don't count):** "representations and warranties"; "equity" meaning stockholders' equity; "identity" in identity management, brand identity or identity theft; "inclusion" in an index; "belonging to"; "diversified" revenue or portfolio; **international sales, supply chains or offices on their own.** Selling in Asia isn't cross-cultural connection.

### Theme 4: Entrepreneurship (15%)

**What it means:** the company helps **other people** start and grow their own ventures: founders, small businesses and independent creators. It does **not** mean the company's own innovation.

**What counts:** tools, payments, lending or marketplaces for small businesses and independent sellers; ways for creators to earn money; programs supporting small or new suppliers; startup accelerators and founder funds; entrepreneur training programs.

| Score | What the 10-K shows | Illustration |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Nothing related | — |
| 1 | Only the company's own innovation, or general language | "Entrepreneurial culture"; R&D spending; its own new product launches |
| 2 | One specific practice | A small-business supplier program |
| 3 | Several specific practices, or a named program with details | A named grant program with the number of small businesses funded |
| 4 | Small businesses or independent creators are a major customer group, with measurable data | Reports the number of small merchants on its platform |
| 5 | Enabling founders, small businesses or independent creators is the core business | A marketplace for independent sellers, or small-business software or lending as the main business |

**Traps (these don't count):** "innovation" and "innovative"; "entrepreneurial spirit"; "joint venture"; "startup costs"; "small business" mentioned only as a risk or competitor.

### What the agent outputs for each company

```json
{
  "ticker": "XXXX",
  "sector": "Information Technology",
  "sections_read": ["Item 1"],
  "themes": {
    "creativity":       {"score": 0, "level_reason": "one sentence naming the ladder level matched", "quotes": []},
    "community":        {"score": 0, "level_reason": "", "quotes": []},
    "cross_cultural":   {"score": 0, "level_reason": "", "quotes": []},
    "entrepreneurship": {"score": 0, "level_reason": "", "quotes": []}
  },
  "thin_filing": false
}
```

The **code**, not the agent, does three things: verifies the quotes, calculates the final score out of 100, and ranks companies within each sector.
