I. Letter to Shareholders 
Looking back on 2025, Taiwan’s economy demonstrated resilience in the face of tariffs imposed 
by U.S. President Donald Trump. Performance was particularly strong in the second half of the year. 
On the external demand front, robust growth in AI and emerging techno logy applications drove 
substantial increases in exports of electronic components, information and communications 
technology products, and audio -visual equipment, resulting in a 3 1.8% year -on-year increase in 
merchandise exports. Imports grew by more than 26.8% due to strong demand driven by exports and 
investment, as well as robust demand from manufacturers for raw materials and capital equipment. 
Investment was another highlight alongside external trade. Supported by strong demand for emerging 
technology products and sustained corporate investment momentum, imports of capital goods and 
production of investment goods both recorded significant growth. Other investment-related indicators 
also demonstrated robust performance. Overall, the economic growth rate in 2025 rose to 8.68%. In 
the capital markets, Taiwan’s equity market delivered outstanding performance in 2025. Although the 
first half of the year experienced volatility due to tariffs, listed companies reported strong revenue 
and earnings growth, driven by AI -related busines s, with performance continuing to be revised 
upward in the second half. In addition, the U.S. Federal Reserve’s three consecutive rate cuts 
beginning in September created a more accommodative financial environment, further supporting 
market momentum. As a result, the market grew by 25.7% for the year, marking the third consecutive 
year of gains exceeding 20%. 
In 2025, the Company reported a consolidated net profit after tax of NT$30.056 billion (with net 
profit attributable to owners of the parent company amounting to NT$30.058 billion). Earnings per 
share reached NT$1.74. Excluding the provision for foreign exchange valuation reserve of NT$5.92 
billion and certain non-recurring items from prior years, profits in 2025 reached a record high. The 
Company’s principal subsidiaries in life insurance, banking, securities, ventur e capital, and asset 
management maintained strong operating performance and delivered solid results throughout the year. 
Among the subsidiaries, KGI Life's net profit after tax was NT$15.8 billion; if excluding the impact 
of a one-time provision of 30% of its pre-tax net profit to the foreign exchange volatility reserve, the 
full-year profit would have exceeded NT$20 billion.  KGI Life achieved a year -on-year growth of 
over 30% in first -year premium, continuously accumulating Contractual Service Margin (CSM), 
while maintaining a stable investment portfolio in asset allocation and actively strengthening the 
foreign exchange price fluctuation reserve. The company also expanded its equity and fixed-income 
positions to enhance recurring yields. These efforts are expected to support stable policy profits and 
investment returns following the adoption of IFRS 17 in 2026. KGI Securities capitalized on strong  
momentum in domestic and international capital markets, driving growth in brokerage services, sub-
brokerage, and wealth management fee income. Supporte d by gains from financial investment 
operations, derivatives trading, and underwriting activities, KGI Securities’ net profit after tax 
increased by 13.5% year-on-year to NT$11.5 billion, reaching the second-highest level in its history. 
KGI Bank delivered a record -high net profit after tax of NT$6.8 billion due to strong business 
momentum that drove growth in net interest income and wealth management fee income. CDIB 
Capital Group focused on investments in high-tech industries and the provision of private equity fund 
products, expanding assets under management and contributing to increased management fee income. 
KGI SITE continued to strengthen balanced and market value ETFs, with total assets under 
management exceeding NT$310 billion, a record high . Looking ahead, KGI Financial Holding will 
continue to advance its “ONE KGI” strategy to further integrate group resources and deepen 
collaboration across its “banking, securities, and life insurance” businesses. By strengthening wealth 
management capabil ities an d optimizing asset allocation effectiveness, and leveraging its solid 
financial foundation and integrated resource advantages, KGI Financial Holding is committed to 
growing alongside its customers and the market, progressing toward sustainable, long -term 
profitability and new milestones.

after the board of directors sets the record date for the distribution of the preceding 
year’s dividend after the financial report is approved by the shareholders at the 
annual general meeting. The number of dividends paid in the year of issuance and 
the number of dividends received in the year of recovery are calculated based on the 
actual number of days the preferred shares are outstanding in that year. If the 
Company has a surplus after the year-end final accounts, it should first pay taxes 
and make up the losses accumulated from preceding years. After setting aside the 
legal reserve and setting aside or reversing the special reserve as required by law, if 
there is any remaining balance, it may first distribute the dividends for the year to 
which the preferred shares are entitled. The Company has the discretion to distribute 
dividends on the preferred shares. If the Company has no or insufficient surplus to 
distribute dividends on the preferred shares in its annual accounts, or if the 
distribution of dividends on the preferred shares will cause the Company’s capital 
adequacy ratio to fall below the minimum requirements set by law or by the 
competent authorities, or if there are other necessary considerations, the Company 
may resolve not to distribute dividends on the preferred shares and the preferred 
shareholders shall not dissent. The preferred shares are noncumulative, and any 
undistributed or under-distributed dividends will not be accumulated and deferred in 
coming years.

aforementioned rate, the preferred shareholders shall not participate in the 
distribution of earnings and capital surplus in cash and capitalization of common 
shares.

2. Board Diversity and Independence:  
(1) Diversity of the Board of Directors: 
The election of the company's directors follows the candidate nomination mechanism as stipulated in Article 16 of the company 's Articles of 
Incorporation. To promote the sound development of the board's structure and functions, the company's "Corporate Gov ernance Best Practice 
Principles" clearly state that the board should consider diversity and independence. The selection of directors should comprehensively consider 
the company's operations, business model, and development needs, focusing on the "basic qualifications" of board members (for example, gender, 
age, nationality, culture, and ethnicity) and their professional knowledge and skills, as well as experience in economic, environmental, and social 
issues, and shareholder opinions. To achieve the ideal objectives of corporate governance, director s should generally possess the necessary 
knowledge, skills, and qualities required for their duties, such as operational judgment, accounting and financial analysis, management 
capabilities, crisis management, in dustry knowledge, international market perspective, leadership, decision -making, and risk management 
knowledge and abilities. Additionally, to ensure the objective execution of duties by independent directors, their consecutiv e terms should not 
exceed three. 
At the Company's Annual General Shareholders Meeting on June 13, 2025, the election of the 9th Board of Directors was completed, with the 
term of board of directors from June 13, 2025 to June 12, 2028. The Board consists of nine members, including three independent directors and 
one female individual director. Independent directors account for 33%, and the female director account for 11%. The Chairman serves as the 
chairperson of the Board and does not concurrently serve as the General Manager or any other senior management position. The members have 
extensive experience and expertise in fields such as finance, venture capital, asset management, mergers and acquisitions, financial accounting, 
information technology and information security, digital transform ation, e-commerce, international legal affairs, and cross -border experience. 
The average age of the Board members is 66 years  old, the average tenure is 4.6 years, and the consecutive tenure of all independent directors 
does not exceed three terms (the ave rage tenure of the three independent directors is 2.8 years). Two directors concurrently serve as executive 
managers, accounting for 22%, and there are no spousal or second-degree kinship relationships among the directors. In addition, the Company’s

[page 26]
directors place emphasis on the diversification of industry experience among Board members. The objective is that, for each term of the Board, 
there shall be at least one director with work experience in each of the banking, insurance, and securities industries. The 9th Board of Directors 
has two, two, and three directors respectively whose industry experience aligns with the aforementioned diversification goals. 
The Compnay’s directors are selected based on the principles of diversity. In addition to emphasizing gender balance, the company's business 
development needs, the directors' professional qualifications, industry experience and overall suitability are comprehensively considered to 
ensure that the board of directors possesses diverse perspectives and professional capabilities. The 9th board of directors h as added one female 
director, demonstrating the Company's ongoing efforts to increase participation in decision-making. Due to intense competition for female talent 
with financial backgrounds in the market, the number of female directors on the Company's board of directors currently does not reach one-third. 
However, looking at the group as a whole,  19 out of the 40 directors and supervisors in the first-tier subsidiaries are female, accounting for 48%, 
significantly exceeding one -third. Furthermore, all first -tier subsidiaries are led by female general managers, demonstrating the high level of 
participation of female professionals in the group's corporate governance and operational decision-making, fully showcasing that gender diversity 
is deeply rooted in the group's culture and governance structure. To further strengthen the gender diversity of the Company's board members and 
enhance female participation in corporate governance and operational decision-making, the Company has taken proactive measures. On February 
20, 2023, the board of directors approved the revision of the company's 'Corporate Governance Best Practice Principles,' expl icitly stating that 
the composition of the board should include female directors. In the future, t he Company will continue to actively recruit female talent with 
professional knowledge, skills, and experience to join the board, and progressively promote the goal of achieving one-third of the board seats by 
gender, to concretely implement gender diversity and fairness.

3.2 External evaluation of the Board's performance 
Frequency The Board shall conduct at least one external Board of Directors evaluation every three 
years. 
Period September 1, 2022, to August 31, 2023. 
Scope Board of Directors and Functional Committees. 
Method External performance evaluations are entrusted to independent professional organizations or 
a team of experts and scholars. 
Content 
The Company engaged Ernst & Young (EY) Advisory to perform the external evaluation of 
Board performance in August 2023. The following results of the evaluation were reported to 
the Board on November 20, 2023: 
1.  Category: Structures, people, and processes and information of the Board of Directors and 
Functional Committees. 
2.  Content: The Board of Directors and Functional Committees structures and processes, 
Board of Directors and Functional Committees members, corporate organization, roles 
and responsibilities, behavior and culture, director training and development, supervision 
of risk control, and supervision of reporting, disclosure, and performance. 
3.  Method: Interviews with individual Board directors/Functional Committee members, 
self-evaluation questionnaires, and document review. 
4.  Criteria: Basic (complying with the basic requirements of the competent authority and 
relevant regulations); advanced (complying with the basic requirements of the competent 
authority and relevant regulations and having a set of defined and effective prac tices or 
proactive improvements in the performance in an aspect); and benchmark (not only 
performing beyond the basic requirements of the competent authority, and relevant 
regulations but also setting an example equivalent to the benchmarking model). 
5.  Result: Based on the comprehensive evaluation, the performances of the Board of 
Directors in "Board of Directors and Functional Committee structures", "people", and 
"process and information" were “benchmark”, “advanced”, and “benchmark”, 
respectively. 
6.  The external experts proposed 5 suggestions, which aim to help the Company, the Board 
of Directors, and the Functional Committees continuously improve and become an 
industry model in terms of corporate governance and performance. 
(1)   It is recommended to optimize specific management goals for the diversity policy of 
Board members, such as the number of independent directors, the number of female 
directors, and the increase of non -executive directors which are superior to 
regulatory requirements. 
(2)  It is recommended to continuously assist the Board of Directors through functional 
committees in responding to the Company's development goals, external regulatory 
trends and various emerging risks to ensure the appropriateness and effectiveness of 
overall mid- and long-term risk management and resource allocation. 
(3)  The Company has established a Nomination and Performance Evaluation 
Committee, and adopted a director candidate nomination system. It is recommended 
that the Company, in addition to continuing to maintain the aforementioned 
benchmarks, can also gradually build a talent pool of directors through diversified 
channels to strengthen nominations and talent succession planning. 
(4)  It is recommended to design advanced learning and development programs for 
directors with different expertise and provide course content and further training 
hours that are better than those required by the competent authorities, so that 
directors can not only strengthen their own professional competencies but also 
strengthen other professional knowledge. 
(5)  It is recommended to continuously evaluate the impact of ESG response measures 
(such as green finance, inclusiveness projects, etc.) on the Company's business and 
financial performance. 
7. In response to the recommendations from external experts, the Company has not only 
maintained its “Excellence” practices but has also implemented several enhancement 
measures. These include appointing one female director to the 9th term Board of Directors

[page 53]
and enhancing board diversity by incorporating directors with professional expertise in 
law, finance, and information security. To further strengthen governance, KGIF 
consolidated its functional committees and increased the proportion of independent 
directors (five out of six functional committees are fully involved by independent 
directors, who represent a majority of committee members). In addition, KGIF established 
a director talent pool and introduced a self-assessment checklist for director qualifications 
to reinforce the nomination and qualification review mechanism.  The Company also 
designed advanced training programs for directors and conducted KGI Group -wide 
director training programs covering diverse topics, resulting in average training hours 
exceeding regulatory requirements. In 2025, the average training hours reached 11.8 hours 
for re -appointed directors (nearly twice the regulatory requirement) and 16 hours for 
newly appointed directors (1.33 times the regulatory requirement).  Furthermore, KG IF 
regularly aligns ESG initiatives with corporate strategy, reports ESG performance to the 
Board of Directors and discloses the results in its ESG reports, thereby enhancing 
corporate governance and strengthening stakeholder trust.

2. Market risk is gauged by estimating the current and

several measurements, including position cost,

profit and loss, and potential loss estimated based on

profit and loss impact of position under extreme 
market conditions of significant financial events.

probability of default, loss given default, exposure,

other risk factors. In addition to monitoring and 
controlling the exposure by imposing daily credit 
limits, the Company also enhanced early warning 
mechanism to identify signs of financial distress or 
deterioration in borrower creditworthiness.

5. Venture capital industry 
The development of Taiwan's venture capital industry can be traced back to the 1980s. In the 
early days, the government enacted relevant investment incentive regulations, providing a 20% 
investment tax credit for investments in venture capital enterprises. In addition to announcing the 
"Regulations for the Management of Venture Capital Enterprises" in 1983, laying the legal 
foundation for Taiwan's venture capital industry. Meanwhile, the Ministry of Finance established 
a seed fund with a capital of NT$800 million in 1986, and invested in TSMC, successfully 
guided foreign venture capital funds and expertise into Taiwan, laying a critical foundation for 
Taiwan's venture capital ecosystem. Since 2013, the venture capital industry has gradually been 
incorporated into the national overall industry and innovation policy framework. Through cross-
ministerial cooperation with the Ministry of Education, the Ministry of Economic Affairs, and 
the National Development Council, the government has established an integrated support system 
covering academic and research basic research, industry-academia collaboration, incubation 
acceleration, and funding matchmaking to systematically assist the growth of startups.

5.6.1 Risk management framework and policies of the Company and its 
subsidiaries 
1. Risk management framework 
The Company has a set of defined authorities to manage risk. Its risk management organization 
and framework are composed of the Board of Directors, senior management, the Risk 
Management Committee (and its sub-committees) and three lines of defense. 
The Company's risk management adopts a three lines of defense framework. The first line of 
defense is responsible for each operational unit to identify, manage, and respond to the risks of 
their respective operational activities. They must ensure that relevant risk management and 
control procedures are effectively implemented to comply with the Company’s risk management 
policy and various risk management regulations, thereby achieving effective risk control. The 
second line of defense is an independent risk management unit responsible for planning and 
managing the company's risk management system, supervising subsidiaries to establish and 
adhere to risk management, monitoring the appropriateness of risk exposure and the 
effectiveness of risk control mechanisms, and providing overall risk management information 
required by senior management (including the Risk Management Committee) and the board of 
directors. The third line of defense is the audit unit, which is responsible for reviewing the 
establishment of various risk mechanisms and auditing the compliance and implementation of 
these mechanisms.
