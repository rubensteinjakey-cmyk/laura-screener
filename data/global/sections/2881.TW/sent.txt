1. Letter to Shareholders
Dear Shareholders,
Looking back at the global economic landscape in 2025, U.S. President Donald Trump spearheaded various 
economic and trade reforms since taking office. Among these, tariff policies have significantly impacted 
international trade and supply chain strategies. Coupled with fluctuating tax rates, implementation timelines, 
and exemption scopes, these measures have contributed to notable shifts in the global political and economic 
environment. Taiwan’s economic performance has been boosted by the AI technology boom. In 2025, Fubon 
Financial Holdings’ net income reached NT$120.94 billion, with earnings per share of NT$8.37, leading the 
sector for the 17th consecutive year.
Beyond deepening its presence in Taiwan, Fubon Financial Holdings has steadily forged a regional network 
and developed innovative financial models through cross -industry alliances. Based on the foundation of its 
own advantages, we strive to integrate peers and cross-industry resources to enhance our financial service 
strength so as to provide diversified financial services for our customers. In the meantime, we are injecting 
innovative energy through FinTech, integrating financial holdings’ resources, covering banking, insurance, and 
asset management services, as well as creating a diversified financial service platform and extending our reach 
to other regions in Asia. The merger between Fubon Asset Management and Jih Sun Securities Investment 
Trust was completed in April 2025, contributing to growth in the investment trust assets under management. 
We will continue to seek various cooperation opportunities in the future. In addition to improving the 
operations of the existing overseas businesses and focusing on Asia, Fubon Financial Holdings will continue 
to evaluate M&A and investment opportunities as we move steadily closer to our vision of becoming one of 
“Asia’s first-class financial institutions”.
Financial Sector Leader in EPS 17 Years in a Row
Looking back at 2025, shifts in U.S. economic and trade policies posed significant challenges to the traditional 
global political and economic systems. The implementation of reciprocal tariffs placed significant pressure on 
Taiwan's traditional industry exports, leading to fluctuations in the financial markets and regulatory measures 
in the housing sector, which in turn suppressed private consumption. However, benefiting from the wave of AI 
development and investment in computing power, there was robust dem and for electronics and information 
communication products, resulting in a substantial increase in overall exports and boosting investments in 
related supply chains. Consequently, Taiwan’s economic growth reached a 15-year high.
In 2025, Taiwan’s economy continued to grow, alongside a vibrant stock market, which resulted in remarkable 
profitability for the financial industry. The subsidiaries of Fubon Financial Holdings continued to perform well. 
Fubon Financial Holdings’ net income reached NT$120.94 billion, with earnings per share of NT$8.37, leading 
all financial holdings companies in Taiwan for the 17th consecutive year! The profit contribution mainly came 
from Fubon Life and Taipei Fubon Bank. Fubon Financial Holdings’ return on assets (ROA) and return on 
equity (ROE) were 0.97% and 12.51%, respectively.
With Fubon Financial Holdings delivering steady profit performance, its consolidated total assets continued to 
maintain growth momentum and reached NT$12.8755 trillion at the end of December 2025, an increase of 7% 
from the end of 2024, and remained Taiwan ’s second largest financial holding company. The subsidiaries, 
including Taipei Fubon Bank, Fubon Securities, Fubon Insurance, Fubon Bank (Hong Kong), Fubon Bank 
(China), and Fubon Asset Management have all achieved record high profits for 2025.

Fubon Life: Financially Sound, Operation Performance Continues to be Stellar
In 2025, due to fluctuations in the global economic market, the New Taiwan Dollar experienced a rapid 
appreciation in the second quarter. This led to significant foreign exchange losses for life insurance companies 
with substantial overseas investments. To mitigate the impact of foreign exchange on profits, Fubon Life 
adjusted its foreign exchange valuation reserve in response to policy changes, ensuring stable operational 
performance. First-year premium reached approximately NT$113.53 billion in 2025, which ranked second in 
the industry. Total premium of NT$367.45 billion also ranked second in the industry. Furthermore, Fubon Life 
prioritized protection -oriented and regular -paid products, achieving first -year premium equivalent of 
NT$51.66 billion. Meanwhile, net income for 2025 was NT$62.65 billion, placing it at the top of the industry. 
To strengthen its capital base, Fubon Life issued US$650 million in subordinated bonds abroad through its 
wholly owned subsidiary in Singapore, which will continue to enhance its financial structure.
Considering policy-driven incentives, Fubon Life has established its presence in the “Kaohsiung Zone of the 
Asian Asset Management Center” as of July 2025, becoming one of the first insurance providers authorized to 
set up business trial operations. Fubon Life has launched life insurance products specifically designed for high-
net-worth and international customers, addressing their needs for asset accumulation and wealth inheritance. 
Furthermore, in collaboration with Taipei Fubon Bank and several financial institutions, Fubon Life offers 
premium financing and policy financing solutions, thereby enhancing the efficiency of asset utilization and 
meeting the demand for asset inheritance, thus seizing opportunities in wealth management.
Overseas, the Fubon Life’s subsidiary in Vietnam emphasizes localized operations, concentrating on the 
fulltime agent channel and developing the market of Taiwanese businessmen. Meanwhile, Fubon Hyundai Life 
Insurance is dedicated to the sales of protectio n-oriented and regular-paid products, while enhancing capital 
management and optimizing asset allocation to increase profitability. The Hong Kong subsidiary is focused on 
developing distinctive products to meet customer needs and is deepening collaboration with affiliated 
companies to leverage synergies.

and Leverages Synergies to Achieve a Record High Profit

After experiencing fluctuations, the global stock market rebounded swiftly in 2025, with the indices rising.

2.3.3 Performance of the Corporate Governance and Sustainability Committee
(1)Composition and Procedures of Corporate Governance and Sustainability Committee
a.The Company’s Corporate Governance and Sustainability Committee has been assembled 
according to the charter, which requires at least 3 Directors, and more than half of whom must be 
Independent Directors. Appointment of committee members is subject to resol ution by the 
Company’s Board of Directors. Currently, the committee consists of all of the Company’s 
Independent Directors plus 1 corporate director representative and 1 internal director.
b.The meetings for the Committee shall be held at least twice each year, and called at any time as 
needed.
(2)Duties of the Corporate Governance and Sustainability Committee
Pursuant to Article 5 of the Company’s “Corporate Governance and Sustainability Committee 
Articles of Incorporation,” the Corporate Governance and Sustainability Committee has the 
following Duties:
a. Supervise the implementation of corporate social responsibility and sustainability matters, and 
assess their implementation.
b. Review, establishment and amendment of the Company’s Corporate Social Responsibility Code 
of Conduct, Corporate Governance Code of Conduct, Business Integrity Code of Conduct, 
Business Integrity Procedures, Behavioral Guidelines and key policies for resolution by the Board 
of Directors.
c. Review the Company’s Articles of Incorporation, Parliamentary Rules for Shareholders’ 
Meetings, Procedures for the Election of Directors and Parliamentary Rules for Directors’ 
Meetings, as amended from time to time, and propose the same to the Board of Dir ectors for 
resolution.
d. Review the enactment and modification of the charters of functional committees, and proposed 
to the Board of Directors for resolution.
e. Supervise and direct the results of the Company’s participation in various corporate governance 
assessments and evaluations, and submission of the Board of Directors’ performance 
assessment/evaluation results to the Board of Directors for discussion and improvement.
f. Discuss the plan for the next year and evaluate the execution of the annual plan and proposal to 
the Board of Directors for resolution and reporting.
g. Evaluate the information collection channel of the Board of Directors, as well as the quality and 
timeliness of the information.
h. Review the corporate governance relationships among the Company and its subsidiaries and 
affiliates.
i. Donation to a related party or a major donation to a non -related party, provided that a public -
interest donation of disaster relief for a major natural disaster is submitted to the next board 
meeting for retroactive recognition.
j. Any other material matter required by the Company or competent authorities.

4.12.2Employee Welfare Measures and Retirement System, and Implementation Status
The Company pays ongoing attention to changes in the external environment and how it affects 
employees' welfare. In addition to complying with government regulations, the Company has also 
implemented welfare measures to meet employees' requirements. These welfare measures include a 
festive bonus, an employee shareholding trust, subsidies (for purposes such as weddings, funerals, 
births, recreational activities, entertainment, children’s education, birthdays, and club activities), 
annual leave, labor/national health/group insurance, health checkups, compassionate pay, 
occupational hazard compensation, general accidental death benefits, internal/external training, 
language courses, professional certification subsidies/rewards, and recognition for long-service 
employees. As for the retirement policy, employees of Fubon Financial Holdings are entitled to 
retirement terms and pension payments equivalent to those stipulated in the Labor Standards Act and 
Labor Pension Act. Furthermore, managers with outstanding service records may be rewarded with 
service credits of up to 10 pension basis points upon retirement.
4.12.3Enforcement of labor agreements and employee rights
Fubon Financial Holdings has always respected its communication with employees, and hosts quarterly 
labor–management meetings as a way to maintain employment relations. The Company was the first 
private financial holding company to sign a collective bargai ning agreement with the union back in 
2015 and has since been negotiating for new agreements with the union upon the expiry of the existing 
agreement. The negotiations were conducted in good faith, and the two parties discussed issues from 
employment terms to welfare measures. Fubon Financial Holdings has introduced several employment 
terms and employee benefits that are more favorable than what the laws require, such as 10 weeks of 
full-pay maternity, 12 days of full -pay funeral leave, birth subsidies, and shareholding trust. The 
Company takes care of all its employees with real action. Taipei Fubon Bank, Fubon Securities, Fubon 
Futures, Fubon Insurance, and Fubon Life have all signed collective bargaining agreements with 
unions, and among all financial holding companies in Taiwan, Fubon Financial Holding has the greatest 
number of subsidiaries with collective bargaining agreements. The Group will continue maintaining 
communication with unions to create a harmonious work environment that benefits both the employer 
and the employees.
Fubon Financial Holdings has implemented “Fubon Financial Holding Co., Ltd. and Subsidiaries 
Guidelines for Handling Employee Grievance” and created a grievance mailbox along with an 
employee grievance section on the intranet, thereby allowing employees to speak out using a variety 
of channels such as the grievance hotline, fax,and e-mail. Dedicated personnel would then be assigned 
to rate the issue in question by severity and handle it according to proper procedures in the employees’ 
best interests.

4.16Information security management
(1) Cybersecurity risk management framework
a. Fubon Financial Holdings created an Information Security Division on April 1, 2019. An 
employee at the vice president level has been assigned to serve as the Chief Information 
Security Officer (CISO), whose responsibilities are to supervise the coordination, planning, 
and execution of cybersecurity tasks within the Group and make regular reports to the Board 
of Directors regarding the execution of the cybersecurity plan and overview of cybersecurity 
risks. Members of the Board of Directors possess the professional cybersecurity background 
needed to supervise governance and are capable of improving the quality of decisions and 
supervisory efforts regarding cybersecurity issues. All subsidiaries have established their own 
cybersecurity departments that are staffed with dedicated personnel to handle cybersecurity-
related matters. At the end of 2025, the financial holding company and its subsidiaries had 92 
cybersecurity personnel in total. They were assigned responsibilities in policy planning, 
system management, technology, protection, etc., and were tasked with various duties ranging 
from system/network security planning and monitoring to protection and maintenance. 
Together, they uphold the cybersecurity management system of the Fubon Group.
b. The "IT and Information Security Governance Committee" has been assembled at the 
Chairperson level to enhance IT and cybersecurity governance throughout the Group. The 
group president serves as the convener of the committee, whereas the rest of the committee 
members consist of the highest ranking IT and cybersecurity officers from the Company and 
its subsidiaries. The committee convenes meetings once every quarter to discuss issues 
concerning the IT/cybersecurity governance strategy, framework, major investment plans, and 
cybersecurity performance, as well as to keep up to date on the latest developments. The 
committee promotes coordination of resources across companies and ensures consistency of 
the decisions made.
c. Considering that cybersecurity incidents are highly time-sensitive and affect several parts of 
an organization, the financial holding company and its subsidiaries have developed their own 
response systems and assembled response teams to ensure the timely recognition of risk 
patterns, quick response times, and minimization of operational impact whenever a 
cybersecurity incident occurs. Furthermore, the Group convenes monthly “Group 
Cybersecurity Joint Defense Meetings” to review subsidiaries' cybersecurity management 
practices, new technology risks, and experiences with incidents so that rolling adjustments can 
be made to refine the protection strategy and responses, thereby improving the resilience and 
stability of the Group's cybersecurity system while enhancing joint defenses across Group 
members.
d. Fubon observes policies of the competent authority and trends of the financial industry, and it 
has assembled an "Implementation Team for Five Main Cybersecurity Functions" to oversee 
critical tasks such as "Assessment of Effectiveness for Cybersecurity Monitoring and 
Protection," "Zero Trust Network Deployment with Enhanced Connection Authentication and 
Authorization Control," and "Supply Chain/Third-Party Service Provider Risk Management." 
Through the integration of group resources and by establishing common management 
standards and assessment systems, Fubon has been able to improve the robustness of its 
cybersecurity governance practices.
e. Fubon continues to maintain conformity with international standards, including but not limited 
to ISO 27001 - Information Security Management, ISO 22301 - Business Continuity 
Management System, and BS 10012 - Personal Information Management System. By adopting 
the P-D-C-A (Plan-Do-Check-Action) management cycle, the Group makes persistent 
improvements to cybersecurity management and ensures that the system functions in ways that 
support business development.

(2) Cybersecurity policy
Fubon Financial Holdings has created a cybersecurity policy, standards, and guidelines that are 
applied consistently throughout the organization to ensure that information is handled according 
to cybersecurity rules. The Company reviews its cybersecurity policy and guidelines annually to 
determine whether they meet the requirements of the business environment and the authorities, 
and conducts assessments on major cybersecurity issues. The financial holding company and its 
subsidiaries have each assembled their own cybersecurity response teams. By hosting regular 
Cybersecurity Joint Defense Meetings, subsidiaries can align their cybersecurity strategies with 
those of the financial holding company. The Group has adopted a cybersecurity joint defense 
system featuring a “centralized management and distributed execution” approach to ensure 
conformity with the prevailing regulations, technologies, organizational policies, and the latest 
business practices. Overall, the cybersecurity system is being duly enforced within the 
organization to minimize cybersecurity risks.
(3) Management solutions and commitment of cybersecurity management resources
a. Fubon exercises supervision over the cybersecurity units of each subsidiary from a group 
perspective. Every single detail from planning, assessment, specification, procurement, system 
implementation to process design has been carried out under the coordination of the financial 
holding company. This approach ensures that all cybersecurity measures conform with 
standards and complement the overall cybersecurity strategy to optimize the performance of 
cybersecurity infrastructures and achieve consistency in management practices across the 
Group.
b. To promote cybersecurity awareness and shape proper cybersecurity culture among 
employees, Fubon requires general staff to undergo at least 3 hours of cybersecurity courses 
each year. All employees are required to complete training on cybersecurity, social
engineering, e-mail security, etc. on a yearly basis, whereas social engineering drills are 
organized every six months to examine employees' alertness and response to simulated attacks. 
Cybersecurity management measures are then optimized based on the outcome of the drills. As 
for cybersecurity specialists, they are required to complete at least 15 hours of professional 
cybersecurity training each year. Through these requirements, Fubon makes every employee a 
part of the protection system. Additionally, a "Cybersecurity and Anti-Scam Newsletter" is 
sent to the financial holding company and its subsidiaries on a monthly basis to raise 
employees' alertness and keep them up to date on the latest cybersecurity threats, scams, and 
preventive measures. With the implementation of the "Cybersecurity Officer" system, each 
department is required to designate a cybersecurity officer whose responsibilities are to: serve 
as the contact window and promoter for cybersecurity matters, assist in the communication of 
cybersecurity policies, report risks, and exercise control over daily routines. Through 
systematic training and cross-department collaboration, Fubon helps these officers improve 
their cybersecurity management capacity in ways that contribute to the resilience of business 
operations.
c. The financial holding company implements a cybersecurity governance roadmap each year 
and makes rolling adjustments in accordance with regulations, internal policies, cybersecurity 
technology trends, and international developments to accomplish main goals such as 
prevention against hacker attacks, removal of vulnerabilities, proper response, compliance, 
business continuity, and customer safety. Personnel, technologies, processes, etc., are specially 
planned to ensure that the roadmap is executed in a manner that aligns with the cybersecurity 
strategy and business targets. Fubon Financial Holdings also pays particular attention to 
adopting international standards on information security management (ISO 27001), personal 
information management system (BS 10012), and business continuity management system 
(ISO 22301) as a means to improve cybersecurity governance practices over time. The 
financial holding company influences how subsidiaries should adjust their cybersecurity 
strategies, and evaluates the cybersecurity efforts of its subsidiaries so that cybersecurity 
management systems of the entire group may work cohesively toward enhancing “overall 
cybersecurity protection.” A set of “cybersecurity performance indicators” have been 
implemented uniformly across the group, which provide the quantitative data needed to

monitor the effectiveness of cybersecurity management efforts, and ultimately ensure that 
cybersecurity risks are manageable while increasing the security and level of trust in financial 
services.
d. Cybersecurity progress is reported to the Group Chairman and Group President on a monthly 
basis. With regard to the overall cybersecurity protection plan, the financial holding company 
observes the vision conveyed in FSC’s Financial Information Security Action Plan to “deliver 
secured, accessible, and uninterrupted financial service” and drives subsidiaries toward 
“enhancing cybersecurity monitoring,” “enforcing data governance,” “strengthening financial 
resilience,” and “supporting joint defense.” Progress is regularly reported to the board of 
directors.
(4) Cybersecurity risks and response measures
a. With regard to cybersecurity strategies and plans, Fubon Financial Holdings has developed 
multi-layer security protection using the five functions (identify, protect, detect, respond, and 
recover) of NIST’s network security framework to plan and implement cybersecurity 
protections that adequately support the digital financial environment. The framework uses a 
combination of antivirus software, malicious mail filters, webpage filters, intrusion detection 
systems, application firewalls, and risk tracking tools. Cybersecurity defense measures have 
also been enhanced for individual networks, information environments, servers, and terminals, 
with cybersecurity monitoring rules and indicators implemented to support risk monitoring. 
Monitoring rules are constantly adjusted, and operational logs of key servers, network 
equipment, and cybersecurity hardware are tracked closely. Cybersecurity reporting and 
response have been duly enforced; information, alerts, records, and intelligence gathered from 
cybersecurity protection equipment are thoroughly observed, analyzed, and associated to 
determine whether a cybersecurity incident has occurred, and any findings discovered are 
converted into valuable intelligence for sharing. The organization keeps track of the latest 
cybersecurity trends and rules around the world, and it makes ongoing optimizations to 
minimize the occurrence of cybersecurity incidents that may pose high risks. Furthermore, in 
light of future financial deregulations and the need to use application programming interface 
(API) for external services, Fubon has developed its own API security monitoring, API 
inventory management, access behavior monitoring, and abnormal data flow detection systems 
to support the identification and control of risks concerning unauthorized access, data leaks, or 
interface misuse. Together, these measures strengthen security protection at the application 
layer and improve the performance of defense measures in place.
b. The terminal detection/response and automated event investigation systems deployed in 
personal computers and servers work in coordination with the network threat detection and 
response system to monitor computer activities and network connections in real time. 
Artificial intelligence is being used to analyze signs of abnormal behavior and to cross-
reference threat intelligence. Any significant finding is transmitted to the visual platform, and 
an alert is triggered in the event of a cybersecurity incident, providing information about what 
happened and the scope of impact. The group implements proper precautions and responses to 
quickly obtain information for post-event recovery. The group also engages independent third 
parties to conduct regular cybersecurity assessments, such as red team assessments, DDoS 
drills, simulated intrusions/attacks, and assessments of cybersecurity in computer systems. 
These assessments examine the completeness and appropriateness of overall computer system 
controls, share the attackers’ mindset, and test the effectiveness of cybersecurity monitoring 
and defenses. Meanwhile, the Company continues to develop responses for new technologies 
and new forms of cyber attack, such as IoT equipment, third-party components, and 
ransomware.
c. Cybersecurity threats are evolving at such a high rate that they are increasing risks across 
supply chains. For this reason, the financial holding company and its subsidiaries make 
rigorous decisions when choosing IT service providers, and adopts measures such as contract 
signing, management practice, supervision, and auditing to ensure that its suppliers meet the 
cybersecurity requirements. Fubon Financial Holdings also states in its contracts the specific 
cybersecurity management mechanisms to be adopted by service providers, and its rights to 
-281-

[page 289]
exercise supervision and audit rights over suppliers to ensure compliance with relevant 
cybersecurity rules. Following recent deregulations on cloud banking services, financial 
institutions are progressively adopting cloud computing and cloud storage technologies as 
means to improve operating efficiency and flexibility. For the security of the cloud 
environment, the Group has developed a cloud security management system that incorporates 
the cloud environment into the cybersecurity governance framework. Actions are then taken to 
ensure that the cloud infrastructure conforms with the cybersecurity and compliance 
requirements. Through the use of cloud native security protection and monitoring, Fubon 
continually identifies potential risks and optimizes parameters and settings to strengthen the 
risk control and management of the cloud environment. In the future, Fubon will evaluate the 
potential of adopting a multi-cloud structure depending on business development 
requirements, and it shall progressively integrate cybersecurity monitoring efforts for cloud 
and non-cloud activities, thereby improving the robustness and consistency of cloud security 
governance practices throughout the Group.
d. The issue of cybersecurity is gathering attention in the business world as new technologies and 
digital transformation grow in popularity. Due to technological advancements, cyber attacks 
are occurring at an unprecedented frequency, speed, scale, and diversity, making cybersecurity 
a real threat in the world. According to online surveys, banking, healthcare, and IT have been 
identified as the three industries most susceptible to cyber attack in recent years, and for this 
reason, the financial holding company has instructed its subsidiaries to purchase cybersecurity 
insurance to ensure recoverability in the event of an attack, and thereby protect customers’ 
assets.
e. In light of the new cybersecurity risks that may arise following the rapid development of 
generative AI technologies, Fubon Financial Holdings has committed additional resources to 
improving its AI governance system. It is constantly observing international risk management 
guidelines as well as trends of financial supervision in Taiwan. Rolling adjustments are being 
made to the cybersecurity management framework to include AI security as part of 
cybersecurity governance. By adopting enhanced risk identification, asset inventory, and 
classification for AI applications, Fubon is able to refine its AI security assessment and control 
procedures and make ongoing optimizations based on the actual performance. With regard to 
the development of an AI cybersecurity strategy, Fubon Financial Holdings will be adopting a 
two-way governance mindset that combines "AI for Security" and "Security for AI." This 
means that AI technology will be adopted as an enhancement to cybersecurity protection and 
as a way to optimize threat detection, behavior analysis, and automated responses to 
cybersecurity incidents for an overall improvement in defense performance. Meanwhile, 
attention will also be directed to ensuring that the AI application conforms to security and 
compliance requirements and meets the supervisory expectations of the FSC, as well as 
regulations, for which Fubon is required to develop security controls and defenses accordingly. 
In the future, Fubon looks forward to progressively introducing AI for technological 
innovation and risk management, provided that cybersecurity protection is in place to ensure 
the stability of business operations.

5.6 Risk Management
5.6.1 Risk management framework and policies
(1) Risk management framework
The Company has established a robust risk management framework. The Board of Directors 
governs and supervises the Company's risk management and control systems, and ensures effective 
implementation of the systems. The board approves the Company's risk management policies and 
reviews key risk management reports. The Board of Directors has an Audit and Risk Management
Committee that assists the board in supervising the Company's risk management practices. A Risk 
Management Executive Committee has been assemb led under the direct supervision of the 
Chairman to review the Company's overall risk management strategy, framework, system, policy 
and the Company and subsidiaries’ risk appetite . The Committee is also responsible for 
supervising credit risk, market risk, operational risk, insurance risk, liquidity risk, assets and 
liabilities risk and capital adequacy risk, reputational risk, emerging risk, and climate change 
management within th e Company and subsidiaries, also regularly submit the Company's risk 
management report to the Audit and Risk Management Committee and the Board of Directors 4 
times per year . 2024 risk management reports were submitted on 2025.03.14, 2025.05.29, 
2025.08.21, 2025.11.20 respectively. A Personal Information Protection Committee has been 
assembled under the Risk Management Executive Committee to supervise protection of personal 
information within the Company and across its subsidiaries to strengthen the management of 
personal data protection.
(2) Risk management policy
The Company has a comprehensive set of risk management policies in place, it is submitted to the 
Board of Directors for approval, including credit risk, market risk, operational risk, insurance risk, 
liquidity risk, assets and liabilities risk, capital ade quacy, reputational risk, s ustainable finance, 
emerging risk and climate change management policies. All subsidiaries are required to comply 
with the above policies so that risks can be identified, measured, responded, monitored and reported 
in an effective manner. The Company also adopts three lines of defense model to ensure full-scale 
implementation of risk management. Risk limits, loss limits, and trade authorities have been 
imposed based on risk attributes. Risk exposures are measured regularly to esta blish points of 
control, indicators and alerts. The Company adopts a combination of quantitative and qualitative 
methods to monitor and manage various types of risk associated with its business activities.

5.6.2 Methods of risk assessment and control
The Company's risk management strategy has been developed based on its overall business plan. It 
involves the use of effective procedures and controls to identify, measure, manage, monitor, mitigate, 
and report the various types of risk, monitor the ongoing effectiveness of avoidance and offset tools, and 
thereby maintain the optimal risk-return profile. Risk information is regularly reported to the Company's 
Risk Management Executive Committee, Audit and Risk Management Committee and the Board of 
Directors.
(1) Credit risk
By utilizing effective procedures and controls, the Company is able to determine possible impacts 
given any changes in business characteristic, complexity, or cyclicality of its portfolio. This enables 
the Company to take pro-active measures against credit risks and monitor its overall asset quality.
The scope of credit risk management covers asset quality, obligor credit risk, issuer credit risk, 
counterparty credit risk, industry credit concentration risk, single -party and single related party 
credit concentration risk, and country risk. A multitude of credit risk and country risk indicators 
and limits have been implemented to monitor and control the various types of risk mentioned above.
(2) Market risk
The Company adopts a market risk management system that effectively manages risks associated 
with interest rates, exchange rates, equity security/commodity prices, and market liquidity. The 
Company has developed internal risk management practices in accord ance with the market risk 
management policy, and has taken a pro-active attitude towards managing market risks.
The scope of market risk management covers: risk assessment and management for non -hedging 
derivatives carried at fair value and securities carried at fair value, valuation for complex financial 
derivatives, VaR calculation, financial market alerts, tail risk management, critical market risk 
event reporting, market liquidity risk measurement, stress test etc.
(3) Operational risk
By implementing effective procedures, controls, indicators and self -assessments, the Company is 
able to identify, measure, control, respond, monitor and report all kinds of operational risks 
associated with its products, services, processes and systems, take pro-active measures to mitigate 
them, and monitor the ongoing effectiveness of avoidance and offset tools.
The scope of operational risk management includes regular reviews on the appropriateness of 
various procedures, controls, indicators and self -assessments that the Company has put in place. 
By monitoring operational risk incidents, monthly/quarterly self -assessments and risk indicators, 
the Company is able to quantify existing or potential operational risks and make timely 
improvements in terms of more robust procedures and enhanced training.
(4) Insurance risk
Insurance risks may include but is not limited to product design risk, product pricing risk, 
underwriting risk, claims risk, catastrophe risk, reserve risk and reinsurance risk. According to 
various risk factors and the nature thereof related to the insurance risk, the risk monitoring 
indicators or risk limits are set, strengthening insurance risk control, and periodically reviewed and 
monitored the indicators and limits.
(5) Liquidity risk
The Company has implemented liquidity indicators to assess and control liquidity risks. Solvency 
and liquidity indicators are monitored on a regular basis and reported to the Risk Management 
Executive Committee to ensure that strategic goals are met.

(a) Credit risk management
Fubon Bank (HK) adopts a credit risk strategy that takes into consideration a number of 
factors such as the overall economy, industry development, global markets and the bank’s 
risk appetite, etc. The relevant credit policies and guidelines have been estab lished based 
upon the operating plan along with assessment, monitoring and reporting procedures, 
whereas concentration limits, large-exposure limits and country limits are set, monitored and 
reported regularly to the Credit Committee. Fubon Bank (HK) adopt s a credit risk system 
that embodies procedures including credit assessment, risk rating, stress test, limit control, 
post-lending management, and collection. In addition, Fubon Bank (HK) has stringent 
verification and early warning system in place to facilitate immediate responses should any 
major credit event occur.
(b) Market risk management
Fubon Bank (HK) has set goals to keep market risks that arise from trading and investment 
activities within acceptable levels.These risks include the impact of changes in the price of 
financial assets on the financial statements at fair value. The trading book exposures 
primarily contributed from foreign exchange and interest rate trading positions while the 
investment portfolio consists of debt securities and interest rate swaps for hedging. To 
effectively manage market risk, Fubon Bank (HK) has formulated a series of control 
mechanisms and market risk management policies that utilize tools such as limit controls, 
reporting system and monitoring procedures. The bank also employs various analysis 
reports, such as sensitivity analyses, stress tests etc. to prevent the bank taking excessive risk 
on profit and equity. Fubon Bank (HK) hedges its exposures with derivatives instruments 
and prudently manages the customer back to back positions in the trading book. Derivatives 
used in investment activities continues to follow the hedge accounting requirements 
specified under HKFRS 9.
