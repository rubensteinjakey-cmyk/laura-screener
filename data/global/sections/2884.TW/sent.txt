Dear shareholders,
In retrospect, the global economy in 2025 demonstrated 
remarkable resilience despite the headwinds of tariff 
disputes and heightened geopolitical risks. Global GDP 
growth maintained a steady pace of 3.2%, bolstered by 
easing inflationary pressures and the initiation of monetary 
easing cycles by major central banks, which spurred a 
recovery in corporate investment and personal consumption. 
Taiwan’s economy exhibited robust momentum, achieving a 
15-year high growth rate of 8.63% in 2025. Driven by the AI 
revolution, the semiconductor and ICT sectors experienced 
rapid growth. Taiwan’s exports surpassed US$640 billion, 
hitting a record high and stimulating private investment. 
Notably, per capita GDP reached US$39,477, surpassing 
Japan and South Korea for the first time—a testament to 
Taiwan’s ability to navigate global trade barriers effectively.
Looking ahead to 2026, the global economy is expected 
to sustain a moderate expansion. AI-driven infrastructure 
and High-Performance Computing (HPC) demand remain 
the primary growth engines. As Western economies 
continue their rate-cut cycles, a potential easing of global 
tariff tensions is expected to revitalize corporate capital 
expenditure and household spending, thereby supporting 
the real economy. As a pivotal player in the global AI 
and semiconductor supply chains, Taiwan’s ICT export 
momentum remains strong, complemented by significant 
progress in trade negotiations. However, challenges remain: 
global supply chain restructuring and spillover effects from 
low-cost Chinese exports in traditional manufacturing may 
exacerbate industrial bifurcation. Consequently, Taiwanese 
enterprises must leverage agile global positioning and 
deepen technological barriers to secure their leadership 
amidst these shifts.
Overall, 2026 will be a year defined by both opportunities 
and challenges. In the face of external uncertainties, E.SUN 
believes that a clear vision and core values are the sources 
of our strength, while precise strategy and execution are the 
key drivers toward sustainable operations. Since our founding 
in 1992, we have remained committed to the vision: "E.SUN 
of Taiwan, E.SUN of the World," aspiring to be the best-
performing and most respected enterprise. Entering the FHC 
2.0 era, E.SUN will continue to expand its footprint, creating 
long-term value for customers, shareholders, employees, and 
society.
A Leap Forward — New Milestones for E.SUN
E.SUN has long focused on its core financial business, 
achieving steady growth for over three decades. In 2025, 
E.SUN FHC reported a net revenue of NT$91.710 billion and 
a record-breaking net profit after tax of NT$34.342 billion, 
representing a 31.4% year-over-year increase. EPS stood 
at NT$2.12, ROA at 0.80%, ROE at 13.05% and Capital 
Adequacy Ratio at 128.02%. Our subsidiaries—E.SUN Bank, 
E.SUN Securities, and E.SUN Venture Capital—contributed 
net profits of NT$32.786 billion, NT$2.61 billion, and NT$120 
million, respectively. Following its integration in July 2025, 
E.SUN Asset Management contributed NT$92 million to the 
group. The ROE for our banking and securities units stood 
at 11.87% and 25.04%, respectively, reflecting exceptional 
overall performance.
Regarding business indicators, E.SUN’s total assets grew 
steadily to NT$4.52 trillion. By the end of 2025, E.SUN Bank’s 
total deposits reached NT$3.78 trillion, a 12.95% increase, 
with foreign currency deposits equivalent to NT$1.30 trillion. 
Total loans amounted to NT$2.63 trillion, up 12.57%. Wealth 
Management net fee revenue rose by 14.15% to NT$15.42 
billion. Asset quality remained pristine, with an NPL ratio of 
0.15% and a coverage ratio of 826.42%. In the securities 
sector, our market share in brokerage and margin trading 
reached 1.78% and 2.84%, respectively, with a total of 45 
lead and co-underwriting deals completed.
In terms of international recognition, E.SUN FHC has been 
selected for the Dow Jones Best-in-Class Index (DJBIC) 
Emerging Markets for 12 consecutive years, ranked among 
the top 5% of global banks for seven consecutive years. We 
also received MSCI ESG AAA rating for four consecutive 
years—setting a record for Taiwan’s financial sector. 
Domestically, E.SUN was honored as a "Sustainability 
Benchmark Enterprise" by the Commonwealth Sustainability 
Citizen Award and received the Gold Award for "Sustainable 
Finance" from Wealth Magazine for 12 consecutive years, 
reflecting high acclaim from prestigious global and domestic 
evaluators.
Strategic M&A to Strengthen FHC Footprint
Since its inception in 2002 through the share exchange 
of our banking, securities, and bills finance units, E.SUN 
FHC has evolved into a comprehensive financial services 
provider. Over the past two decades, we have deepened 
our core banking operations, with total assets exceeding 
NT$4.5 trillion and profits surpassing NT$30 billion by 2025, 
demonstrating excellence in operational performance, 
corporate governance, service quality, risk management, and 
sustainable development.
As the financial landscape evolves, E.SUN FHC is entering 
a new era. Beyond organic growth, we actively pursue 
strategic alliances and M&A opportunities to enhance 
competitiveness. In March 2025, E.SUN FHC successfully 
acquired Prudential Investment Trust (renamed E.SUN Asset 
Management in October). Following the government’s policy 
to establish Kaohsiung as an Asian Asset Management 
Hub, E.SUN Asset Management will serve as a vital product 
engine, integrating with our wealth management and private 
banking sectors to enhance the depth and breadth of our 
services.
Furthermore, with the implementation of IFRS 17 and ICS 
2.0, transparency in the insurance sector has significantly 
improved. This presented a strategic window to introduce a 
life insurance arm as our second growth engine. On January 
23, 2026, E.SUN FHC Extraordinary General Meeting 
(EGM) approved the consensual merger with Mercuries Life 
Insurance with over 85% support. Upon regulatory approval, 
this merger will complete our "three pillars" (Banking, 
Insurance, Securities), pushing our asset scale past NT$6 
trillion and ranking E.SUN among the top five listed FHC in 
Taiwan.

Leveraging Synergies to Achieve Sustainability
In our fourth decade, E.SUN will reinforce its three core 
advantages: Professional Leadership, Service Excellence,

Entering the FHC 2.0 era, E.SUN will leverage its banking

capital, and insurance to generate cross-subsidiary 
synergies. In our dual-axis transformation of technology and 
sustainability, we focus on data analytics, cloud applications,

Integration for a Brilliant Future
Named after the highest peak in Taiwan, E.SUN is dedicated 
to being the finest financial institution—a favorite of our

we have built a "flywheel" of steady growth, evolving into a 
major FHC recognized by investors and society. 
"Integration" will be our central theme for 2026—linking 
information and sharing resources across subsidiaries to 
create a comprehensive financial ecosystem that delivers 
end-to-end solutions. With wisdom and determination, we 
will commit fully to advancing the vision “E.SUN of Taiwan, 
E.SUN of the World.” We extend our deepest gratitude to 
our shareholders for their long -term support, expectations, 
and encouragement, and offer our sincerest thanks and best 
wishes.

4.3 Market and business competitive landscape
4.3.1 Key business area
Business Key business area
Corporate Banking Services
E.SUN is centered in Taiwan, strategically expanding overseas business primarily in Asia to meet the 
cross-border financial needs of customers. Currently, E.SUN operates 35 branches and offices in 11 
countries/regions spanning the range from financial centers, developed countries, to developing 
countries. Looking forward, E.SUN will continue to expand its overseas network with integrated cross-
border services and localized business development to provide customers with comprehensive financial 
services and consistent customer experience.
Wealth Management and 
Consumer Banking Services
E.SUN Bank operates the services primarily in Taiwan. There are 140 domestic branches in total. We 
also develop wealth management services in Hong Kong and Singapore and develop consumer banking 
services in Cambodia and Japan to satisfy the oversea customers' needs.
Credit Card and Payment Services
Taiwan is our main service area, and the UCB subsidiary in Cambodia is also developing local credit 
card and debit card services. E.SUN has also expanded the electronic payment business and 
cooperated with PayPal, Alipay, Razer Merchant Services and HIVEX(PayPay). We continually broaden 
our cross-border payment services to overseas markets such as Europe, America, and the Asia-Pacific 
region.
Securities Services Taiwan is the main service area. As of December 2025, 17 branches have been established, and with 
103 joint marketing offices located at bank branches, there are totally 120 service locations.
Venture Capital Services Taiwan is the main service area, with our office located in Taipei.
Asset Management Services Taiwan is our primary service area, with our headquarters located in Taipei and branch offices in 
Taichung and Kaohsiung. These three locations form a comprehensive service network across Taiwan.
4.3.2 Future opportunity of market
(1) Tracking the inventory adjustment progress : Rising needs of remote work and the supply chain chaos caused high demand 
for industrial components and commodities in manufacturing sector during the outbreak. However, market demand declined 
dramatically, suffering from high inflation and war between Rusian and Ukraine. Consumer electronics such as laptops, 
tablets, phones, panels and traditional manufacturing industries such as bicycles, clothing, and footwear generally have 
inventory depletion issues. Facing the global inflation and high interest rate environment, it is important to keep tracking 
whether the market demand is strong enough to reduce the high inventory level. 
(2) Pay attention to the development of overseas commercial real estate (CRE): post-pandemic work patterns in Europe and 
America have changed, adopting a combination of work-from-home and physical office mode. This has resulted in weakened 
demand for office spaces, declining occupancy rates, and further depreciation of real estate value. Moreover, high interest 
rates have increased the financial burden on CRE operators, leading to a continuous rise in bad debts for American banks. 
Small banks, such as Chen Xiaoming, are also facing refinancing issues. It is necessary to monitor the changes in the CRE 
market, as well as the financial and potential spillover risks it may bring.
(3) The regulatory pressure on net-zero carbon emissions is increasing year by year. Both domestic and international regulations 
on net-zero carbon emissions are gradually becoming stricter. Starting from October 2023, the EU will require high carbon 
emitters to report their carbon emissions, and a carbon border tax will be levied from 2026. COP28 will also include fossil 
fuels in its regulations, requiring a gradual reduction in their use. This will force the transformation of industries such as 
petrochemicals, energy, and transportation. Taiwan also plans to impose a carbon fee in 2025, with high carbon emitters in 
industries such as petrochemicals, steel, aluminum, and chemicals being the primary targets for collection. It is urgent to 
implement carbon reduction plans and conduct carbon inventory checks in the relevant supply chains. The financial industry 
should provide suitable channels for investment and financing to assist industries in their transition to net-zero emissions and 
play a crucial role in this process.
(4) Observing the market changes brought about by the development of AI: Generative AI has contributed to speeding up work 
efficiency, and major companies have gradually incorporated it into their business processes. However, as AI’s output in 
content generation greatly surpasses human capabilities, a wave of unemployment is expected, particularly affecting the 
technology industry, graphic designers, and writers, with knowledge workers also being potentially impacted. Additionally, 
the adoption of AI is most rapid in the technology and electronics industries, and if utilized effectively, it can strengthen a 
company’s competitive advantage. However, the digitization process in traditional industries is generally lagging behind, and 
there is often limited understanding of the benefits of AI. This leads to slower development in AI implementation and raises 
concerns regarding long-term competitiveness.

Business Objective
Corporate Banking
By leveraging digital technologies, E.SUN provides enterprises with a diverse and differentiated range of 
products and services, including corporate financing, syndicated loans, trade finance, cash 
management, cross -border payment solutions, and succession planning advisory. Through the 
integration of domestic and overseas networks, E.SUN continues to expand its financial platform across 
Asia and deepen its presence in local markets, with the aspiration of transforming “Taiwan’s E.SUN” into 
“the world’s E.SUN.”
Consumer Banking
E.SUN focuses on centralized risk control to strengthen our development capabilities. E.SUN provide 
high-quality experiences through digital services and create greater value for physical channels with 
excellent customer service. E.SUN also continually develop cross-industry collaborations and support 
regional revitalization, and self-initiated urban redevelopment to promote social prosperity and co-create 
a sustainable home.
Private Banking and Wealth 
Management
E.SUN adheres to the spirit of "pure as jade, sturdy as the mountain". Based on a people-oriented 
foundation, E.SUN connects and integrates resources across departments and leverages technological 
capabilities to meet customers' needs in asset allocation. Additionally, E.SUN continuously exerts 
positive influence, collaborating with customers and partners to build a sustainable future. From earning 
trust to becoming indispensable, E.SUN strives to be the most distinctive and beloved wealth 
management bank.
Credit Card and Payment
E.SUN Credit Card has focused on important customer segments and planned distinctive card services 
to maintain a leading position in the market for premium customers, civil servants, and other customer 
segments. Furthermore, we are expanding into corporate payments to meet business expenditure needs,

and linked our credit card, electronic payment and personal unsecured loan products to create the E.
SUN payment life ecosystem.
Securities
The brokerage business will focus on high-value client segments, expand its market share in the spot 
market, diversify its product range and grow the size of its sales team. By using data analytics to 
segment customers accurately, it will enhance the functionality of its digital platforms and boost 
marketing momentum, thereby improving customer retention and transaction activity, and building a 
comprehensive financial ecosystem. In underwriting, we provide consultative services, optimal product 
platforms, and customer support to help clients enter the capital market, boosting their competitiveness 
and driving sustainable corporate growth. Additionally, our proprietary trading activities focus on refining 
medium to long-term investment strategies, optimizing returns, and effectively managing risks.
4.3.4 Challenges in the future and in the external environment
(1) Challenges in the future and in the external environment
I. Cross-industry competition and cooperation: The development of new technologies and business models accelerates 
changes in the boundaries of various industries, and the government’s new laws and deregulation promote the 
development of cross-industry competition and cooperation and various ecosystems. Traditional financial business may 
be affected.
II. Volatile global environment and higher geopolitical risk: Major regional economies around the world are competing or 
cooperating, affecting the layout of global supply chains. Providing cross-border financial services is challenging for 
financial institutions.
III. Regulation changes: Regulations on the fields of anti-money laundering(AML), countering the financing of terrorism(CFT), 
personal information protection, global tax reporting standards, and so on are becoming stricter. Companies face the 
increasing cost of legal compliance, which examines companies’ adaptability.
IV. The trend of sustainable development: As global climate change intensifies, the United Nations is tirelessly promoting 
carbon reduction actions, and governments are responding by setting policies and targets one after another. Sustainable 
development has become a crucial subject for business operations.
(2) Countermeasures and E.SUN's competitive advantages
I. Fintech and digital financial development: Continue to promote digital transformation to increase the operational efficiency 
of the organization and create good customer experience; Leverage technology and digital power to develop innovative 
business models.
II. Increase overseas presence and expand cross-border business: Increase overseas locations and expand the service 
network to meet customer needs.
III. Three lines of defense and risk management: Build organizational resilience, take the initiative to identify emerging risks, 
and carry out corresponding management measures.
IV. ESG and sustainable development: Integrating ESG with the financial industry and working with customers and partners 
to exert a positive influence. Benchmark our operation against international best practices and continue to improve 
relevant management measures.

6.3.1 Accounting treatment for impairment of assets
(1) Impairment of financial assets
The Company recognizes a loss allowance for expected credit losses on financial assets at amortized cost (including accounts 
receivables), investments in debt instruments that are measured at FVTOCI, lease receivables, as well as contract assets. 
The Company always recognizes lifetime expected credit losses (i.e. ECLs) for accounts receivables and lease receivables. For 
all other financial instruments, the Company recognizes lifetime ECL when there has been a significant increase in credit risk 
since initial recognition. If, on the other hand, the credit risk on the financial instrument has not increased significantly since initial 
recognition, the Company measures the loss allowance for that financial instrument at an amount equal to 12-month ECL. 
Expected credit losses reflect the weighted average of credit losses with the respective risks of a default occurring as the 
weights. 12-month ECL represents the portion of lifetime ECL that is expected to result from default events on a financial 
instrument that are possible within 12 months after the reporting date. Lifetime ECL represents the expected credit losses that will 
result from all possible default events over the expected life of a financial instrument. 
For internal credit risk management purposes, the Company determines that the following situations indicate that a financial 
asset is in default (without taking into account any collateral held by the Company): 
I. Internal or external information show that the debtor is unlikely to pay its creditors. 
II. When a financial asset is more than 90 days past due unless the Company has reasonable and corroborative information 
to support a more lagged default criterion. 
The impairment loss of all financial assets is recognized in profit or loss by a reduction in their carrying amounts through 
a loss allowance account, except for investments in debt instruments that are measured at FVTOCI, for which the loss 
allowance is recognized in other comprehensive income and the carrying amounts of such financial assets are not reduced. 
Under FSC guidelines, E.SUN Bank should classify credit assets as sound credit assets or unsound credit assets, with the 
unsound assets further categorized as special mention, substandard, with collectability highly doubtful and uncollectable, 
on the basis of the customers’ financial position, valuation of collaterals and the length of time the principal repayments or 
interest payments have become overdue. 
E.SUN Bank made 100%, 50%, 10%, 2% and 1% provisions for credits deemed uncollectable, with collectability highly 
doubtful, substandard, special mention and sound credit assets (excluding assets that represent claims against an ROC 
government agency), respectively, as minimum provisions. Furthermore, the FSC stipulated that banks should make at least 
1.5% provisions each for sound credit assets in Mainland China (pertaining to short-term advance for trade finance) and 
loans for house purchases, renovations and constructions, respectively.Credits deemed uncollectable might be written off if 
the write-off is approved by the board of directors. 
(2) Impairment of property and equipment, right-of-use assets, investment properties and intangible assets other than 
goodwill
At the balance sheet date, the Company reviews the carrying amounts of its property and equipment, right-of-use assets, 
investment properties and intangible assets (except goodwill) for any indication of impairment loss. If any such indication 
exists, the recoverable amount of the asset is estimated to determine the extent of the impairment loss. Corporate assets are 
allocated to the individual CGUs on a reasonable and consistent basis of allocation. The recoverable amount is the higher of 
fair value less selling costs or value in use.
If the recoverable amount of an asset or CGU is estimated to be less than its carrying amount, the carrying amount is 
reduced to its recoverable amount. If asset impairment loss reverses, the increase in the carrying amount resulting from 
reversal is credited to earnings. However, loss reversal should not be more than the carrying amount (net of depreciation or 
amortization) had the impairment loss not been recognized. 
(3) Goodwill
Goodwill (part of intangible assets) from business combination is recorded at acquisition cost and subsequently measured at 
cost less accumulated impairment. 
For impairment test purposes, goodwill is allocated to each CGU that benefits from the synergy of a business combination. 
In testing assets for impairment, the Company compares the carrying amounts of operating segments (CGUs with allocated 
goodwill) to their recoverable amounts on a yearly basis (or when impairment indicators exist). CGUs with goodwill allocated 
in the current year should be tested for impairment before the end of the year. When the recoverable amount of CGUs is below 
the carrying amount, an impairment loss should be recognized to reduce first the carrying amount of goodwill of the CGU and 
then the carrying amounts of other assets of the CGU proportionately. Any impairment loss should be directly recognized as 
loss in the current period, and subsequent reversal of impairment loss is not allowed. 
On disposal of the relevant CGU, the amount attributable to goodwill is included in the determination of the gain or loss on 
disposal.

6.3.4 The objectives and methods of hedge accounting:
The Company designates certain hedging instruments as fair value hedges.
Fair value hedges
Gains or losses on derivatives that are designated and qualify as fair value hedges are recognized in profit or loss immediately, 
together with any changes in the fair value of the hedged asset or liability that are attributable to the hedged risk. The change in 
the fair value of the hedging instrument and the change in the hedged item attributable to the hedged risk are recognized in profit 
or loss in the line item relating to the hedged item.
The Company discontinues hedge accounting only when the hedging relationship ceases to meet the qualifying criteria; for 
instance, when the hedging instrument expires or is sold, terminated or exercised.

2.1.2  Trade-offs in Policies Related to Sustainability and Climate-related Risks and Opportunities
The Board of Directors ensures that sustainability are integrated into the overall corporate business strategy, based on 
recommendations from the Sustainability Committee and the Audit and Risk Management Committee. When reviewing 
the company’s overall strategy, decisions on major transactions, performance targets, and risk management processes 
and policies, the Board incorporates and weighs sustainability and climate-related risks and opportunities. This involves 
evaluating their alignment with E.SUN FHC’s business model and long-term strategy, as well as their contribution to 
achieving the company’s sustainability goals.
2.1.3  Target Setting for Sustainability and Climate-related Risks and Opportunities, and Linkage to Performance 
and Remuneration Policies
E.SUN FHC has established the "Manager Performance Evaluation Policy" and the "Manager Compensation Policy," both 
of which are reviewed by the Remuneration Committee and approved by the Board of Directors. In accordance with the 
performance evaluation regulations, E.SUN FHC conducts assessments of senior executives. The evaluation criteria 
encompass not only operational performance but also non-financial performance, the three lines of defense, and sustainable 
development. Senior executives responsible for specific risks and opportunities are required to set sustainability goals 
linked to their performance appraisals, with the final results determined by the Chairman based on a comprehensive review 
of the aforementioned indicators. Pursuant to the remuneration regulations, E.SUN FHC integrates performance appraisal 
results with the overall compensation framework. Furthermore, the Remuneration Committee is responsible for establishing 
and periodically reviewing policies, systems, standards, and structures related to managerial performance evaluation and 
remuneration.

oversight responsibility, continuously monitoring sustainability policies and risk management to ensure that sustainability and climate-related risks are 
incorporated into the Group’s overall enterprise risk management (ERM) framework.
To ensure the integrity of disclosures, E.SUN FHC has formulated the Directions for the Preparation and Assurance of Sustainability Information Reporting.

and defining clear divisions of responsibility. Furthermore, reporting follows the “E.SUN Financial Holding Co.,Ltd. Subsidiaries Greenwashing Prevention 
Guideline” to prevent the provision of unclear or exaggerated messages, selective disclosure of positive impacts, or claims lacking evidence, thereby 
mitigating greenwashing risks.

3.Strategy
3.1 Strategy for Sustainability and Climate-Related Risks and Opportunities
E.SUN FHC recognizes the critical importance of identifying sustainability and climate-related risks and opportunities. These 
strategic considerations have been fully integrated into the Company's operational processes, forming an indispensable 
component in driving sustainable financial services. In identifying sustainability and climate-related risks and opportunities 
that could reasonably be expected to affect the Company’s prospects, E.SUN FHC refers to the IFRS S2 Industry-based 
Guidance—specifically Volume 15: Asset Management & Custody Activities, Volume 16: Commercial Banking, Volume 18: 
Investment Banking & Brokerage, and Volume 19: Mortgage Finance. These volumes are utilized to determine disclosure 
topics and assess applicability, considering all reasonable and supportable information available without undue cost or 
effort, including data regarding past events, current conditions, and forecasts of future conditions.
E.SUN FHC utilizes climate scenarios annually to assist in identifying climate-related risks and opportunities and to enhance 
climate risk assessment procedures. During the scenario-setting process, the Company identifies potential climate-
related risks and opportunities under various scenarios. By evaluating factors such as operating costs, revenue generation 
capabilities, business continuity, and corporate reputation, E.SUN FHC determines which risks or opportunities may have 
a material financial impact on the Company’s prospects. Furthermore, the Company conducts scenario analysis to assess 
its strategic resilience in the face of these risks. For detailed identification processes, please refer to "4.3.1 Information on 
Inputs and Parameters of Data Sources Used for Climate Risks" and "4.3.2 Impact and Utilization of Climate-Related Scenario 
Analysis in Risk Identification."

3.3.3  Climate Transition Plan
In alignment with global decarbonization trends and Taiwan’s 2050 Net-Zero goal, E.SUN FHC launched the "E.SUN 
Low-Carbon Transition Plan" in 2025. This plan establishes a transformative strategic framework that shifts the focus 
from mere carbon management to transition empowerment. Recognizing that 99% of our carbon emissions originate 
from our investment and financing portfolios, the Company has evolved beyond traditional emission control mindsets. By 
benchmarking against international standards, we have developed a "Transition Maturity" management mechanism. This 
systematic industry classification and grading methodology assists enterprises in their journey from high-carbon to low-
carbon status along a net-zero pathway, while simultaneously enhancing the climate alignment of the Company’s asset 
portfolio.
Regarding mitigation pathways, E.SUN FHC has received validation for its Science Based Targets (SBT). We achieved our 
2030 commitment to reduce Scope 1 and Scope 2 greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions by 42% ahead of schedule in 2024. 
On the operational front, we focus on renewable energy, green buildings, and energy-saving measures. Our goals include 
achieving 100% green building certification for all self-owned buildings by 2027, and 100% renewable energy usage across 
all domestic locations by 2030 and global locations by 2040. For Scope 3 emissions, which represent our most significant 
impact, the Company has set specific SBT targets for categories including electricity generation project finance, commercial 
real estate loans, electricity generation industry loans, non-manufacturing sector lending, high-carbon sector lending, and 
securities investments.
E.SUN FHC’s Low-Carbon Transition Plan has progressed to a new stage of "Empowering Industries through Finance." 
Through a standardized classification and grading framework, science-based reduction targets, advanced carbon data 
systems, and diverse financial instruments, we are progressively driving the climate transition of enterprises, industries, and 
supply chains. This initiative accelerates the progress of Taiwan and Asia toward a net-zero future. For detailed information, 
please refer to the "E.SUN Low-Carbon Transition Plan" in the Sustainability section of the E.SUN FHC official website.

4. Risk Management
4.1 Sustainability-Related Risk and Opportunity Management Framework
In line with its Enterprise Risk Management (ERM) framework, E.SUN FHC has established the Risk Management Policy 
and Guiding Principles through processes including risk identification, measurement, monitoring, reporting, and response. 
In addition, the Company has formulated the Climate-Related and Environmental Risk Management Policy to integrate 
sustainability related risks (including climate change) into its existing risk management framework.
• The Board of Directors serves as the highest governance body and is responsible for reviewing and approving risk 
management policies, procedures, and frameworks, ensuring alignment between risk management and business 
strategies, and allocating resources to ensure the effective operation of risk management. The Risk Management 
Division is responsible for fostering and implementation, including the formulation of risk policies, setting risk appetite, 
analyzing sources and categories of risks, regular risk reporting, coordinating risk management among subsidiaries, 
and strengthening the execution and oversight of risk management. The audit division is responsible for assessing the 
effectiveness of risk management to ensure the proper implementation of policies, procedures, and control measures.
• Sustainability and climate change risks are incorporated as one of the key risk categories, alongside credit risk, market 
risk, liquidity risk, operational risk, legal risk, money laundering and terrorism financing risk, and information security risk. 
Climate risk is classified into physical risks and transition risks, and may be assessed through dependency and impact 
analyses. Through the three lines of defense for internal control, the Company implements the identification, measurement, 
monitoring, and response to climate and environmental risks. In particular, E.SUN FHC strengthens climate scenario 
analysis and stress testing to assess the potential impacts on operations, products and services, and financial assets, 
and formulates effective mitigation strategies to reduce risks. Each year, the Risk Management Division conducts climate 
scenario analysis and stress testing with reference to the “Plan for Climate Change Scenario Analysis of Domestic Banks” 
methodology, and regularly reports the results to the Board of Directors.
• When conducting business activities, E.SUN FHC, in accordance with the Risk Management Policy and Guiding Principles, 
consider risks that may affect the Company’s strategic objectives and ensure that such risks are controlled within an 
acceptable range. Each subsidiary is required to establish risk management procedures that meet the Company’s 
requirements based on its respective business characteristics and to regularly report relevant information to the Risk 
Management Division. Through a rigorous risk management framework, risk management is embedded into daily 
operations and management processes, thereby supporting the achievement of strategic objectives and enhancing 
management effectiveness.
4.2 Identification of Sustainability-Related Risks and Opportunities
With the adoption of IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards this year, E.SUN FHC has established a new process 
for identifying and assessing sustainability and climate-related risks and opportunities. The Sustainable Development 
Committee oversees and guides risk management, while the Sustainable Development Department coordinates with relevant 
units to perform risk identification and assessment. This process results in a comprehensive list of sustainability and climate-
related risks and opportunities, followed by in-depth analysis and action planning. The procedures are as follows:
4.2.1  Collection
.SUN FHC compiles a potential risks and opportunities list by referencing international standards (e.g., SASB Standards), 
ESG ratings, industry benchmarks, and stakeholder engagement. In evaluating SASB compliance, the Company references 
industries including Commercial Banking, Consumer Finance, Asset Management & Custody Activities, Investment Banking 
& Brokerage, and Mortgage Finance. Furthermore, the Company considers Volumes 15, 16, 18, and 19 of the "IFRS S2 
Industry-based Guidance." By assessing the applicability of "Disclosure Topics" from these standards, the Company 
analyzes potential risks and opportunities to compile an evaluation list. Scenario analysis has not yet been utilized in this 
process.
3.4.3    Adjustments to Financial Position for the Next Reporting Period
Regarding the aforementioned sustainability and climate-related risks and opportunities that have a financial effect on the 
current period, it has been assessed that none are expected to have a significant risk of resulting in a material adjustment to 
the carrying amounts of assets and liabilities within the next reporting period in the related financial statements.
3.4.4    Disclosure Requirements Under Conditions of Exemption from Providing Quantitative Information
Certain disclosures in Section 3.4.1, "Financial Effects of Sustainability and Climate-Related Risks," and Section 3.4.2, 
"Financial Effects of Sustainability and Climate-Related Opportunities," are presented as qualitative descriptions. The basis for 
this determination is explained below:
3.4.4.1 Reasons for Not Providing Quantitative Information
E.SUN FHC has determined that the degree of measurement uncertainty involved in estimating these effects is so high 
that the resulting quantitative information would not be useful. Therefore, qualitative descriptions are provided to enable the 
primary users of general-purpose financial reports to understand these impacts.
3.4.4.2 Qualitative Information Provided in the Absence of Quantitative Data
Where E.SUN FHC is unable to provide quantitative information in its sustainability and climate-related financial disclosures, 
qualitative information is disclosed to ensure the comprehensiveness of the Company's reporting and to facilitate stakeholder 
understanding.
3.4.4.3 Quantitative Information on Combined Financial Effects
For certain instances where quantitative information regarding the current or anticipated financial effects of a specific 
sustainability or climate-related risk or opportunity is not provided, the Company also does not provide quantitative 
information on the combined financial effects of that risk or opportunity with others or with other factors. This is based on 
E.SUN FHC's assessment that such combined quantitative information would not be useful.

4.2.2  Assessment
To determine the materiality of risks and opportunities, E.SUN FHC primarily evaluates their potential financial impact and 
likelihood of occurrence. The financial impact is categorized into five levels—Very Low, Low, Medium, High, and Very 
High—with corresponding scores from 1 to 5, while the likelihood is divided into five 20% increments (ranging from 0-20% 
to 80-100%) and also scored from 1 to 5. Materiality is ultimately determined by the average product of these two scores, 
establishing a quantitative basis for identifying significant sustainability and climate-related issues.
E.SUN FHC conducts identification workshops where the IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Task Force completes 
questionnaires based on the above definitions. This evaluates the severity and probability of each risk and opportunity to 
establish a prioritization hierarchy.
4.2.3  Prioritization
Statistical results provide quantitative data for ranking sustainability-related risks and opportunities. Subsequently, senior 
management conducts a qualitative assessment based on these findings to filter and finalize the sustainability and climate-
related risks and opportunities with the greatest potential impact on the Company.
4.2.4  Monitoring
E.SUN FHC has established specific targets and metrics to identify and measure the progress of sustainability and climate-
related risks and opportunities (refer to Section 5, "Metrics and Targets"). The Company regularly tracks achievement 
progress and reviews the effectiveness and appropriateness of these targets for continuous improvement. To enhance senior 
management’s focus on climate issues, a portion of variable remuneration for executives is linked to climate-related risk and 
opportunity metrics. For further details, please refer to Section 2.1.3, "Target Setting for Sustainability and Climate-Related 
Risks and Opportunities, and Linkage to Performance and Remuneration Policies."
4.3  Management Processes for Climate-Related Risks
In accordance with the principles of the Task Force on Climate -related Financial Disclosures (TCFD) and the Taskforce 
on Nature-related Financial Disclosures (TNFD), as well as the Company’s Climate-Related and Environmental Risk 
Management Policy, the Company implements a rigorous climate and environmental risk management framework and related 
processes. Climate change -related risks are integrated into the existing risk management system to strengthen management 
and reporting mechanisms for climate change and environmental risks. Based on the results of climate and environmental 
risk assessments, the Company adopts risk -based management measures. In alignment with the three lines of defense 
for internal control and the respective roles and responsibilities of each unit, climate and environmental considerations are 
further embedded into operational decision -making, management systems, and business processes, thereby enhancing the

4.4 Management Processes for Other Sustainability-Related Risks
E.SUN FHC has established systematic management mechanisms for the two material non-climate risks identified this year: 
"Data Breaches or System Interruptions Caused by Cyberattacks" and" Financial Loss or Reputational Damage Resulting 
from External Fraud." These mechanisms ensure that risk control remains consistent with the company's overall strategy and 
sustainability goals. Both risk categories operate under the Enterprise Risk Management framework, following procedures for 
identification, assessment, prioritization, and monitoring. Decision-making and resource allocation are implemented through 
governance mechanisms to maintain operational resilience and market trust.
Regarding cybersecurity risk, E.SUN FHC identifies risks primarily based on the expansion of emerging businesses, updates 
to laws and regulatory requirements, and external cybersecurity incidents or internal anomalies. This ensures coverage of 
potential risk sources such as hacker attacks, malware, system vulnerabilities, and human error. During the assessment 
stage, E.SUN FHC first confirms the value of information assets to determine the required level of protection and logically 
allocates differentiated control measures within resource constraints. Subsequently, threat scenario and vulnerability 
analyses are conducted to evaluate impacts further. By calculating Value at Risk (VaR) to quantify the overall risk level, 
the company completes risk prioritization and resource allocation. Risk treatment plans are formulated and submitted to 
the Information Security Management Sub-committee for review before implementation, with continuous oversight by the 
Information Security Management Committee.
As digital finance becomes more prevalent and fraudulent techniques continue to evolve, the impact of fraud-related risks 
on operational performance and brand reputation has become increasingly significant. E.SUN FHC integrates regulatory 
notifications, peer and external intelligence, internal transaction monitoring, and customer feedback to keep pace with new 
fraud patterns and identify potential vulnerabilities. In the fourth quarter of 2024, in response to regulatory requirements, a 
permanent dedicated anti-fraud organization was established. Supervised by executives at the Executive Vice President 
level or above, this organization has built a rigorous control mechanism encompassing pre-incident prevention, mid-incident 
detection, and post-incident monitoring. The Risk Management Department of the Retail Banking Risk Management Division 
serves as the dedicated anti-fraud unit for E.SUN Bank. The Head of the Retail Banking Risk Management Division acts 
as the Convener of the Financial Anti-Fraud Task Force, while the Department Head of the Risk Management Department 
serves as the Executive Secretary. The Task Force consists of three working groups: Alert & Prevention: Composed of 
planning or management personnel responsible for suspicious and anomalous transactions. Awareness & Outreach: 
Composed of marketing and communication personnel fr om relevant channels. Tech-driven Anti-fraud: Composed of 
personnel from the Technology Team.

4.5.2 Climate Scenario Analysis
4.5.2.1 Significant Areas of Uncertainty Considered in Assessing Climate Resilience
In assessing climate resilience, the Company considers key areas of material uncertainty, including changes in policies and 
regulations, breakthroughs or delays in low carbon technologies, and shifts in market demand and consumer preferences. 
These considerations enable the Company to remain flexible when addressing climate related risks and opportunities, adapt 
to different scenario developments, maintain resilience, and ultimately achieve sustainable development objectives.
The key areas of material uncertainty are described as follows:
1. Changes in Policies and Regulations:
Efforts to advance global and domestic 2050 net-zero emissions policies may increase in response to evolving political 
and economic conditions, geopolitical developments, progress of international agreements, and the impacts of climate 
change. Related regulatory requirements and carbon pricing mechanisms may also be implemented at an accelerated 
pace, potentially exerting material impacts on corporate business models. 
2. Breakthroughs or Delays in Low carbon Technologies:
The development of low carbon technologies—such as renewable energy, carbon capture, utilization, and storage (CCUS), 
and hydrogen energy—will directly affect the progress of industrial transition and the reduction in carbon emissions, and 
may alter the structure and costs of energy supply. 
3. Changes in Demand and Consumer Preferences:
With the expansion of ESG investment trends and the growth of the green finance market, customer demand for low 
carbon products and sustainable financial services is changing rapidly. The Company must adjust product strategies in a 
timely manner in response to market developments to meet expectations for sustainable value.
4.5.2.2 Scenarios Aligned with International Agreements
In conducting short, medium, and long term climate scenario analyses across the value chain, the Company references 
the Net Zero scenario of the Global Energy and Climate Model released by the International Energy Agency (IEA) in 2025. 
This scenario is aligned with the objectives of the Paris Agreement, the latest international climate agreement, and aims 
to limit the increase in global average temperature to within 1.5°C by the end of the century. In addition, in accordance 
with the “Domestic Bank’s Application of Climate Change Scenario Analysis” methodology, the Orderly Net Zero scenario 
adopts the Net Zero 2050 scenario (Phase IV) of the Network for Greening the Financial System (NGFS), as well as the SSP1 
1.9 scenario from the Sixth Assessment Report (AR6) of the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC). These 
scenarios are collectively used to assess the potential financial impacts arising from physical and transition risks under a 
pathway that limits global temperature increase to within 1.5°C by the end of the century.

5.2.1.4 Measurement Approach, Inputs, and Assumptions Used to Measure Greenhouse Gas Emissions
Regarding the disclosure of greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions, E.SUN FHC is committed to enhancing the comparability 
of its reporting with international peers while strictly adhering to the measurement requirements of IFRS S2. Consequently, 
starting from 2025, the Company has conducted its inventory for Scope 1 and Scope 2 GHG emissions in accordance with 
the internationally recognized "Greenhouse Gas Protocol: A Corporate Accounting and Reporting Standard (2004 Edition)" 
(hereinafter referred to as the "GHG Protocol Corporate Standard").
Measurement Approach, Inputs, and Assumptions
For investees not included in the consolidated financial statements, E.SUN FHC adopts the Operational Control Approach 
under the GHG Protocol Corporate Standard, as the Company does not have the power to control significant operating 
decisions of these entities. GHG emissions measured using this approach serve as the basis for the Company’s metrics and 
targets.
E.SUN FHC applies the Emission Factor Method using activity data, emission factors, and Global Warming Potential (GWP) 
values. Key inputs include the Ministry of Environment’s 2024 emission factors, the Ministry of Economic Affairs’ latest 
electricity carbon intensity, and IPCC AR6 GWP values. These inputs and assumptions remained unchanged during the 
reporting period.
E.SUN FHC calculates emissions following the GHG Protocol and PCAF (2022) standards, utilizing a mix of external 
databases, internal data, and estimations. Guided by SBTi, the scope covers investments (listed equity, corporate bonds, 
REITs) and financing (power generation projects, commercial mortgages, and corporate loans). We will continuously expand 
this scope in alignment with the latest PCAF methodologies and the 2023 Domestic Banking Scope 3 Practical Manual.

5.2.6  Internal Carbon Prices
Carbon reduction has become a global trend. As a vital tool for driving sustainable development, the Internal Carbon Pricing 
(ICP) mechanism not only heightens overall corporate carbon awareness but also effectively internalizes carbon costs, 
serving as a critical foundation for carbon reduction strategies and resource allocation. By implementing ICP for Scope 1 
and Scope 2 emissions, E.SUN FHC enhances the transparency and monitoring of carbon data, encouraging all business 
units to actively participate in decarbonization. This mechanism further optimizes investment decisions and operational 
efficiency, accelerating the low-carbon transition. This initiative aligns with international standards and demonstrates E.SUN 
FHC's commitment to implementing Science Based Targets (SBTs) and achieving its Net-Zero vision.
After establishing a stable and precise carbon emission information base, E.SUN FHC has set an internal carbon price 
of US$ 100 per tonne (as a shadow price). This price is used to calculate carbon costs, which are then disclosed to each 
business unit. Moving forward, E.SUN FHC will pre-allocate carbon fee budgets for each unit and integrate them into 
management accounting and performance evaluations. This will continuously strengthen carbon management awareness 
among all employees and ensure the effective implementation of carbon reduction targets.
5.2.7  Remuneration
E.SUN FHC links corporate sustainability targets to executive remuneration by setting a specific weight for sustainability-
related performance. This approach effectively incentivizes the implementation of corporate-wide sustainability actions. For 
further details, please refer to "Section 2.1.3: Target Setting for Sustainability and Climate-related Risks and Opportunities, 
and Linkage to Performance and Remuneration Policies."
5.2.5  Capital Deployment
In 2025, E.SUN Bank issued four Sustainability Bonds totaling approximately NT$ 3.6 billion. The proceeds were allocated 
to Green Investment Projects, including renewable energy, biodiversity, agriculture and forestry conservation, greenhouse 
gas reduction, and water conservation. Additionally, funds supported Social Impact Investment Projects focused on essential 
service needs, affordable housing, socioeconomic development, and empowerment. E.SUN also utilized its own capital to 
install solar photovoltaic equipment on the roofs of its office buildings to increase the proportion of self-generated renewable 
energy. Simultaneously, the bank has been phasing out old official vehicles and replacing them with Hybrid Electric Vehicles 
(HEVs) to reduce operational emissions.
