Business growth and entrepreneurship 
JPMorganChase believes that small and mid-sized businesses are the backbone of 
the U.S. economy. Their success strengthens communities by creating new jobs and 
driving local economic growth. We support small and mid-sized businesses at every 
stage of growth by offering expertise, connections and opportunities. In the U.S., 
the Firm serves approximately 7.4 million small business customers24 and more than 
35,000 middle market clients25. We continued to expand access to capital for small 
businesses, scale executive coaching and technical assistance, and strengthen 
commercial corridors — efforts that we plan to reinforce through ADI. 
 
Expanding access to capital 
and core banking services 
From traditional banking needs like checking, savings and credit cards to more 
complex services like payments products, lending and cash management, 
JPMorganChase supports entrepreneurs and their businesses at every stage 
of growth. 
JPMorganChase aspires to help small businesses access the capital and banking 
services needed to support their growth and long-term resilience. Business Banking 
serves small business owners in the contiguous U.S., working to empower them to 
start, run and grow their enterprises. We have continued to expand access to core 
banking services for small businesses, and in 2025, Business Banking provided 
$3.2 billion in loans and lines of credit. We have provided financing and business 
banking solutions to help small businesses expand production capacity, invest in 
product solutions and meet growing demand from customers, helping position them 
for long-term growth. 
Additionally, the Firm’s Commercial Banking (“CB”) team provides a bridge for small 
businesses as they enter the middle market. In 2025, CB extended more than 
$41 billion in new loans, refinancing and renewals to middle market clients. CB also 
supports middle market clients with industry expertise and tailored financial 
solutions, including digital banking and payments, credit and financing, international 
banking, advisory services and more. 
The Firm’s philanthropic capital26 also helps entrepreneurs and small businesses 
start, grow and hire in their communities by strengthening community lending 
capacity, providing technical assistance and reinforcing local small business support 
systems. In 2025, we committed more than $90 million in philanthropic capital to help 
small businesses overcome barriers to growth. We work with local community 
lenders, including Community Development Financial Institutions (“CDFIs”) and 
Community Development Corporations, and other local business-serving 
organizations to help entrepreneurs access coaching and financing, build networks 
and grow sustainably. 
 
Creating opportunities 
for business growth 
We aim to help small businesses access the networks and mentorship necessary for 
sustained growth, resilience and knowledge-sharing. 
The Firm supports entrepreneurs through additional leadership and peer-learning 
programs. For example, our Coaching for Impact program helps business owners 
grow their businesses through one-on-one consulting and executive coaching, 
accessible on-demand education and banking solutions. As of year-end 2025, 
the Coaching for Impact program had approximately 80 local Senior Business 
Consultants across 45 cities to provide coaching and host educational events, 
community workshops and business training seminars. In 2025, Senior Business 
Consultants have coached more than 10,000 small businesses in communities across 
the U.S. In addition to our Coaching for Impact program hires, in 2025, we added 
more than 130 business bankers to support our clients through our vast 
branch network. 
Our CB team also supports programs that help business leaders grow their 
businesses. In 2025, CB and Syracuse University’s D’Aniello Institute for Veteran & 
Military Families supported approximately 70 business leaders selected for 
CEOcircle, a yearlong peer accountability training program co-founded by the Firm 
and tailored to help veteran and military spouse executives scale their mid-sized 
businesses. Since its launch in 2021, more than 70% of participants in CEOcircle have 
reported gaining new business opportunities as a direct result of their involvement in 
the program. 
The Firm aims to continue to scale our efforts to mentor and graduate more small 
business owners and hire more Senior Business Consultants and small business 
bankers across the U.S. As small businesses further implement AI-driven solutions, 
research and analysis, the Firm plans to continue providing resources and practical 
guidance to help navigate opportunities in this changing space. 
The Firm provides businesses with access to customers and networks that help 
enable growth, such as by increasing opportunities to participate in public and 
private procurement. 
JPMorganChase strives to build strong business relationships, including with 
our suppliers. We source from a broad and diverse range of suppliers, engaging with 
those that directly provide products and services to meet our needs as we serve our 
clients, customers, communities and employees. We take an inclusive approach to 
procurement, and in addition to engaging large businesses, we consider qualified 
small and diverse businesses27 to expand access to opportunities for them to be 
considered for engagements28. We leverage the strength of our procurement 
organization to assist small businesses in meeting the challenges and minimum 
requirements of doing business with large corporations.

Financial health and wealth creation 
JPMorganChase believes advancing consumer financial health is important for the 
well-being of the individuals and communities we serve. As such, the Firm seeks to 
offer products, services and solutions that enable individuals to build stability, 
resilience and sustainable wealth. These offerings leverage our reach and expertise, 
extending to consumers across urban and rural areas — including those in LMI 
communities. In the U.S., we support consumers at every stage of their financial 
journey to address their near-term needs, while maintaining financial foresight to 
facilitate their path to realizing the American Dream. 
Access to mainstream 
financial services 
We seek to meet consumers’ financial needs in the U.S. by providing an array of 
solutions and experiences, being present in our communities, and offering 
financial education to equip them with the information they need to succeed. 
Access to financial services begins by providing consumers affordable and easy entry 
into the banking ecosystem. At JPMorganChase, we offer Chase Secure Banking℠, a 
low-cost, Bank On certified checking account. Developed by the Cities for Financial 
Empowerment Fund (“CFE Fund”), the Bank On National Account Standards are a set 
of guidelines and best practices designed to improve access to safe and affordable 
banking services. Secure Banking helps customers spend only what they have, with 
no overdraft fees, no fees on most everyday transactions like money orders and 
cashier’s checks and an ability to waive the monthly service fee based on qualifying 
deposits. Secure Banking connects customers to the broader Chase ecosystem, 
including access to thousands of fee-free ATMs, our mobile app and banker support 
in person or by phone. After opening a Secure Banking account, customers reported 
saving an average of more than $45 per month in fees, which if annualized would 
amount to more than $500 per year, compared to experiences with alternative 
financial services36. Since launching in 2019, we have opened approximately 3 million 
Secure Banking accounts and continue to explore ways to further strengthen the 
offering, including additional ways to waive the monthly service fee. 
We maintain a presence in our communities through our bank branch network, which 
provides tailored and relevant support geared to local consumer needs. Since launching 
our market expansion initiative in 2018, we have opened more than 1,000 
branches, surpassing 5,000 Chase branches in the U.S. and representing the largest 
branch network in the country. We are the only U.S. bank with branches in all 
48 contiguous states. 
As part of our bank expansion in the U.S., the Firm has added more than 100 branches 
in smaller cities and towns. For example, we built on our efforts in Iowa in 2024 by 
continuing to expand access to consumer, business and community banking services 
for customers in the state. In 2025, we opened our first branch in the city of Ames, the 
Firm’s 10th branch in the state. Additionally, the Firm’s Iowa-based Community 
Manager conducted more than 95 community events, including financial health 
workshops aimed at equipping customers with insights and skills to make more- 
informed financial decisions. We plan to continue this localized focus. For example, in 
Alabama, we are working to grow our branch network, increase hiring to provide 
more local support, host tailored financial health workshops and cultivate 
relationships with nonprofits and local institutions. 
We seek to provide additional resources to local communities. Since 2019, we have 
opened 19 Community Centers and hired more than 160 Community Managers 
across the U.S. These Community Centers provide a space for financial health 
workshops, skills development courses and events hosted by local community 
organizations and partners. Our Community Managers are responsible for building 
and nurturing relationships with customers, key community leaders, nonprofit 
organizations and small businesses at the neighborhood level. In 2025, our 
Community Managers hosted over 19,000 community events, with over 70% of these 
events taking place at non-branch locations such as local schools, nonprofit 
organizations, corporate centers and virtual events. Since 2021, we have hosted more 
than 48,000 financial health workshops, reaching almost 1 million attendees. 
Additionally, in 2025, we expanded our Community Manager coverage to all 
48 contiguous states. 
The Firm’s philanthropic efforts further our aim of making mainstream banking more 
accessible and beneficial. In 2025, we approved a multiyear $2 million grant to the 
CFE Fund to accelerate the Bank On movement, marking our strategic ten-year 
relationship with the organization. With our support, the CFE Fund has strengthened 
cross-sector coalitions and encouraged the financial services sector to launch 
accounts that meet the financial needs of LMI communities. Bank On has reported 
growth to include nearly 100 coalitions since 2006, with over 500 certified accounts 
across the U.S. since 2015.

Housing access and affordability 
Housing is the foundation of thriving communities — it anchors families, supports 
employment and wealth building, and fuels local economic growth. JPMorganChase 
helps support communities by financing the development of housing and working to 
expand access to homeownership. We plan to continue supporting efforts to improve 
housing affordability for renters and buyers as part of ADI. 
The Firm aims to help grow and preserve the supply of affordable housing that 
meets th
e needs of our communities through a variety of tools and capital 
solutions. We participate in federal programs that incentivize investment in 
housing and are using our knowledge and expertise to help create new solutions 
that will help meet this important need for individuals and families across the 
income spectrum. 
In 2025, the Firm extended more than $10 billion in debt and equity to help create and 
preserve

participation in federal programs such as the Low Income Housing Tax Credit 
(“LIHTC”) and Historic Tax Credit (“HTC”) which are designed to help incentivize 
investment in affordable housing. 
Our specialized teams provide expertise and solutions to help meet a range of rental 
housing 
needs. Community Development Banking delivers loans, investments and 
specialized financing to developers and organizations serving LMI families 
nationwide. Our Workforce Housing Solutions team develops financing structures 
that help fill the gap between market-rate housing and traditional affordable housing 
and create new supply for middle-income workers. Additionally, our Tax Oriented 
Investments team invests in LIHTC-supported projects that can help revitalize 
communities. Through our Agency & Institutional Capital business, we deliver 
affordable housing lending solutions across Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac programs. 
In 2025, the Firm gained a Freddie Mac Targeted Affordable Housing license, which 
authorizes us to originate specialized multifamily loans designed to create or 
preserve affordable rental properties.

Attracting the best talent and 
investing in the future workforce 
Our recruitment efforts aim to attract and hire highly qualified talent in all roles and

Removing barriers to employment 
We aim to expand our talent pipeline by recruiting from a wide range of channels and 
industries, and by engaging individuals with diverse skills, backgrounds and 
experiences. For example, in 2025, approximately 80% of jobs posted for 
experienced hires in the U.S. did not require a bachelor’s degree, emphasizing the 
value of a candidate’s skills. As part of our inclusive hiring strategy, we actively work 
to remove barriers to employment through initiatives such as the ReEntry Program, 
virtual call centers (“VCC”), military and second chance hiring. 
Since its inception in 2013, the JPMorganChase ReEntry Program has helped 
reintegrate professionals into the workforce across all regions where we operate. 
The program offers experienced individuals who have taken an extended career 
break with the support and resources to relaunch their careers. Through comprehensive 
coaching, ReEntry fellows receive guidance on professional identity, cultivating a 
success mindset, networking and work-life balance — all aimed at increasing their 
chances of securing permanent positions in the corporate world. 
Our VCC program is designed to help address critical business needs while 
expanding customer service career opportunities in communities where talent exists 
but access to traditional call center sites has been limited due to geographic or career 
mobility constraints, including for military spouses and bilingual talent. The program 
currently operates in Detroit, Baltimore, Atlanta and Tucson48. Our VCC program has 
created more than 450 jobs across the U.S., offering competitive pay, benefits and 
career advancement opportunities. 
JPMorganChase co-founded the Veteran Jobs Mission in 2011 to expand career 
opportunities for veterans and military spouses. The Firm has helped grow the 
coalition to more than 300 companies, and in 2025 the coalition surpassed one 
million veteran hires. Since 2011, JPMorganChase has hired approximately 21,000 
veterans. In addition, our Military Pathways Development Program provides veterans 
with resources, training and mentorship through two one-year rotational roles, 
designed to help position participants for leadership opportunities at the Firm, 
accelerate their development and provide employment opportunities within our 
various lines of business. 
Our Second Chance Agenda strives to remove barriers to employment and provide 
economic opportunities for people with criminal records whose histories have no 
bearing on the job they are hired to perform. This initiative is designed to create 
broader opportunities for individuals with criminal records, both within our own 
company and across the communities we serve. We work to expand second chance 
hiring by encouraging other employers to consider qualified, talented candidates who 
may have a criminal record, and by supporting common-sense policy solutions that 
can help Americans access a second chance to participate in the economy. For more 
information on our Second Chance Agenda policy efforts in 2025, see page 49.

Equal Employment Opportunity classifications and is presented for U.S. employees 
who self-identified, and information on gender is presented for global employees who

Operating Committee and the Board of Directors reflects all such members. 
Information on LGBTQ+ and veteran statuses is based on all U.S. employees, and all 
members of the Operating Committee and the Board of Directors. Information on 
disability status is based on all U.S. employees and all members of the Operating

employment decisions.
