HONORING AND PRESERVING LOCAL CULTURE 
Host has supported the creation of Hale Kukuna — which is the largest Hawaiian 
cultural center in any resort in Hawai’i. Located at Fairmo nt Kea Lani, Maui, Hale 
Kukuna, meaning “house of enlightenment,” was conceptualized in collaboration 
with the resort’s cultural advisory board and lineal descendants of Palauea, the 
land on which Fairmont Kea Lani resides.
This immersive center honors the rich traditions of Hawai’i while fostering  
meaningful connections between visitors, employees and the local community. 
Hale Kukuna is supported by a dedicated Hawaiian culture department,  
comprising five full-time Native Hawaiian employees. Each team member brings 
expertise in traditional practices, including lei making and fishpond restoration. 
They lead immersive workshops and programs that deepen cultural awareness, 
and enable guests to interact with indigenous folkways, including learning how 
to play the ukelele. Hale Kukuna also houses a curated collection of Hawaiian 
books, artifacts and artwork, with plans to showcase historical pieces from Hale 
Ho‘ike‘ike and Native Hawaiian artisans.
Fairmont Kea Lani also supports the restoration of the Waihe‘e Coastal Dunes 
and Wetlands Refuge — a 277-acre site of profound cultural and ecological  
significance. Once home to thriving ancient Hawaiian villages, an inland  
fishpond, and several heiau, this initiative seeks to recreate a traditional system 
that once sustained both the environment and Native Hawaiian communities.

INTEGRATION INTO RENOVATION PROJECTS
Prior to design and development, Host considers social and economic impacts — such as pedestrian walkways, public 
art, traffic flow and adjacent uses — and works closely with local regulators to address any community concerns. 
During the design and development phase, we consider local community and regulatory input in addition to  
property-specific considerations. During construction, we consider a range of local community impacts, which may 
include noise, dust control, traffic, safety and historical and cultural preservation. For new developments, reviewing 
best practices within the LEED® process helps us to identify additional opportunities to create positive local  
community impacts during design, implementation and occupancy.
DISASTER RELIEF 
Each year, communities where Host owns hotels are challenged by hurricanes, wildfires, tsunamis and other severe 
weather events. To support disaster relief, we leverage our long-standing partnership with the American Red Cross 
and our property teams to engage with local organizations to provide critical, real-time community support. In some 
cases, this includes using our properties as a place for emergency shelter and distribution of food and other supplies.
In 2025, Host contributed to organizations — including World Central Kitchen and the Los Angeles Regional Food Bank — 
to support Los Angeles wildfire relief. Host properties, including Marina del Rey Marriott, also offered refuge to displaced 
residents during the devastating wildfires. We also made contributions to support national and local organizations — 
including Community Foundation of the Texas Hill Country — following catastrophic floods in Central Texas.
